# SEO research behind CLAUDE.md (November 2026)

This is a record of the research used to write the page playbook. Re-check it once a year or after a major Google or Bing guideline change.

## Scope

- 14 search queries covering every page topic in the keyword map: custom clothing manufacturer, low MOQ, T-shirt, polo, heavyweight and oversized tees, hoodie, private label, startups, Bangladesh, cut and sew and full package, samples, how to find a manufacturer, plus official guidance.
- 39 pages read in full: 8 official guideline pages and 31 manufacturer pages, directory pages and buyer guides.
- About 45 more domains reviewed from search results only.

## Official guidance read in full

| Source | What it changed in the playbook |
|---|---|
| Google, Optimizing your website for generative AI features | AI search is still SEO. No llms.txt, chunking or special schema needed for Google. Mass pages for query variants or fan-out count as scaled content abuse. Unique first-hand content wins. |
| Google, Spam policies | No doorway or location pages. No scaled content. No keyword stuffing, including blocks of city or country names. Accordions and screen-reader text are fine. |
| Google, Title links | No length limit, but titles get cut to the device width. Brand at the start or end with a delimiter. The main title should be the most prominent text, like the first H1. No boilerplate titles. |
| Google, Snippets and meta descriptions | No length limit. Unique per page. Specific, with product details. No keyword lists. |
| Google, Image SEO | Use `<img>`, descriptive file names, alt text that describes the image in context, no stuffing. Images near relevant text. Same URL for a reused image. |
| Google, Crawlable links | Descriptive, concise anchor text. No "click here". Link to every important page. Avoid over-linking. `nofollow` only for sources we do not trust. |
| Google, Helpful, reliable, people-first content | Self-assessment questions. Google has no preferred word count. Do not change dates to look fresh. Explain who made the content, how and why. |
| Google, Organization structured data | Full Organization markup on the home or about page only. Logo at least 112×112px and visible on white. |

Also checked: OpenAI crawler documentation (OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT search, GPTBot for training, ChatGPT-User may ignore robots.txt), Bing Webmaster Guidelines 2026 (Copilot uses the Bing index, IndexNow recommended, prompt injection treated as abuse) and Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance report.

## What the top pages do well

1. State MOQ, sample time and bulk time as numbers (Hawthorn, Fashion Soul, Bomme CMT).
2. Explain the process as numbered steps with what the buyer approves at each step.
3. Compare models in a table: CMT vs full package (Bomme), sample types with purpose and cost (Visionise), GSM bands (Printful).
4. Lead with buyer risks: reorder drift, weak prints, risky first orders, small orders ignored (Modaknits).
5. Tell the buyer what to send for a fast quote.
6. Informational guides carry a named author, a date, a table of contents, comparison tables, an FAQ and citations (Maker's Row, Bryden article, Uphance, SaleHoo).

## What almost every competitor gets wrong (our advantage)

1. No prices. Only 1 of 31 pages read gave unit prices. We publish 300 and 5,000 piece prices.
2. Conflicting numbers. MOQ or lead time differs between hero, FAQ and meta (Bomme, Bryden, SaleHoo, Maker's Row). We pull every number from `company.ts`.
3. Copy-paste errors between sibling pages ("each hoodie" on a polo page, Bryden).
4. Empty FAQ answers (The Studio, Fabriclore), placeholder counters showing "0+", links to "#" (Fashion Soul), leaked drafting notes (Modaknits).
5. Keyword stuffing in copy and alt text (Zega, Fashion Soul, Siatex, Fabriclore, Bryden alt text).
6. Doorway-style location pages and long country or city link lists (Siatex, Fabriclore).
7. Superlatives with no proof: "world-class", "leading", "best", "trusted by 1,000+ brands".
8. Stale dates and copyright years that disagree with the page.
9. Information shown only inside images, so search engines and screen readers miss it (Visionise timeline chart).
10. Two URLs for the same topic (Bomme private label), typos, too many pop-up CTAs.

## Batch 1 product pages (hoodie, joggers, pants, shorts, woven shirts)

Searches: joggers and sweatpants manufacturer, pants and trousers manufacturer, shorts manufacturer, woven shirt manufacturer (about 60 results reviewed). Read in full: modaknits.com (sweatpants), makersrow.com (shorts list, shirt list), thestudio.com (cargo pants), ecojersey.com (activewear joggers), plus the hoodie pages from the first round (Bryden, The Studio, Modaknits, Siatex).

Findings used:
- Buyers of joggers worry about waistband roll, baggy knees, set colour mismatch and reorder drift. None of the pages read publish a price.
- Shorts pages list types and fabrics but give no inseam guidance. We added inseam and liner choices.
- Shirt lists admit they give no guidance on collars, cuffs, interlining or fabric. We added collar, cuff, placket, fusing and pattern-matching detail.
- Cargo and trouser pages leave out waistband, closure, sizing system and testing. We added a sizing-system table and a "how trousers are priced" note.
- Copy-paste errors seen again: hoodie text on a joggers page (Eco Jersey), empty FAQ answers (The Studio).

## Batch 2 product pages (jacket, activewear, leggings, tracksuit, streetwear)

Searches: jacket manufacturer (bomber, coach, windbreaker), activewear manufacturer, leggings manufacturer (squat-proof), tracksuit manufacturer, streetwear manufacturer (about 85 results reviewed). Read in full: zegaapparel.com (leggings guide), thestudio.com (jackets), brydenapparel.com (activewear), modaknits.com (custom tracksuit guide), thygesenapparel.com (streetwear manufacturer list).

Findings used:
- Leggings guides give conflicting spandex and GSM figures for squat-proof fabric. We give approximate blends and a weight range, and say we stretch-test and squat-test the proto rather than promise a number.
- The jacket page read lists types but leaves out lining, cuffs and zips, and its FAQ answers are empty. We added shell, lining, trim and branding detail and an honest line on padded and waterproof jackets.
- Activewear pages promote bonded seams and certificates. We only list what we do in-house: flatlock and cover-stitch, mesh panels, sublimation, DTF, stretch screen print.
- Tracksuit buyers worry most about top and pants not matching. We built the page around one fabric lot, one lab dip and stripe alignment.
- Streetwear lists value washes and fades. We do not do these in-house, so the page says so and focuses on fit, weight and print.

## Batch 3 product pages (dress, skirt, loungewear, kids and baby, uniform)

Searches: dress manufacturer small batch, loungewear and pyjama manufacturer, kids and baby clothing manufacturer and safety, uniform and workwear manufacturer, skirt manufacturer (about 85 results reviewed). Read in full: compliancegate.com (US children's clothing rules), makersrow.com (loungewear manufacturers). Search results also covered UL (children's apparel testing), CPSC-related guidance, Custom Ink and Lands' End (uniform programmes) and jinfengapparel.com (dress pages).

Findings used:
- Kids' rules from the compliance page: drawstring limits on children's upper outerwear (16 CFR 1120), tracking labels on garment and packaging, third-party testing by a CPSC-accepted lab, a children's product certificate, lead and phthalate limits and separate sleepwear flammability rules. The kids page explains the design limits we follow and states that the brand chooses the lab and issues the certificate.
- Loungewear lists give no MOQ or prices and skip tech pack needs, fabric minimums and payment terms. Our page covers fabrics, piping and set matching, and treats top and bottom as two styles towards the minimum.
- Uniform pages from retailers sell branded blanks with no minimum. Our angle is made-to-spec programmes with colour matching and reorders from files kept on record. Certified hi-vis and flame-resistant garments are stated as outside our range.
- Dress and skirt factory pages are thin and repetitive, with heavy keyword stuffing on dozens of near-identical style pages. We kept one page each and added fit, lining, length and closure detail.
- Found another copy of our old content: cloth-2ou.pages.dev/skirts-manufacturer. Added to the owner's delete list.

## Batch 4 (underwear, women's clothing, plus size, modest fashion, merch)

- Underwear: buyers ask about waistband elastic, pouch shape, modal vs cotton-spandex and multipacks. Jacquard logo elastic comes from an elastic mill with its own minimum, so the page says it is confirmed on the quote. We state that we make soft bralettes only (no wired or moulded bras) and do not knit socks.
- Women's clothing: ranking pages are mostly directories. Our angle is fit (women's block, fit sample in the middle size, second fit round) and a capsule-first start. Dresses, skirts, plus size and modest have their own pages and are linked, not targeted.
- Plus size: the key buyer pain is straight grading from a small base size. The page explains a separate plus base size and the fit points (bust, upper arm, armhole, front length). Prices for extended sizes are shown separately on the quote.
- Modest fashion: abayas, kaftans, tunics, lengths per size and opacity. Machine embroidery only; hand beading and stone work are stated as not done.
- Merch: competitors are mostly print on demand with no minimum. Our angle is cut and sew bulk merch with your own fit and labels, planned back from a tour or launch date. Merch is a new URL, not in the legacy list. The page asks buyers to own the rights to their artwork.
- Correction (26 November 2026): cloth-2ou.pages.dev is the live sdfltd.com project, not a duplicate. dhaka-bd.pages.dev, the-hug.pages.dev and test-5rx.pages.dev no longer answer (robots.txt unreachable). Earlier fetches returned cached copies. Nothing needs deleting.

## Batch 5 (school uniform, scrubs, cotton, linen, bamboo)

- School uniform: buyers are mostly uniform suppliers and school groups. Their worries are shade continuity across years, a wide size range from infant to adult, crest quality and arriving before term. Typical published MOQs run from a few hundred to several thousand per style. The page uses owner-approved prices for polo, kids' tee, crewneck, joggers and shorts. Blazers are not claimed.
- Scrubs: buyers are medical apparel brands and distributors. Key points: poly-cotton vs polyester-rayon-spandex stretch, pockets and pen slots, colour fastness through hard washing, fibre labels matching the mill spec. No antimicrobial or fluid-resistant claims. Old `/medical-uniform-manufacturer/` now redirects to scrubs.
- Cotton: competitors explain combed vs carded and ring-spun vs open-end. Our angle is shrinkage control before cutting and honest organic wording.
- Linen: untreated linen often shrinks about 4% on the first wash (Parachute care guide). We use mill pre-washed linen, since we do not garment-wash.
- Bamboo: FTC requires "rayon (or viscose) made from bamboo" labelling and says the fibre keeps no antimicrobial properties. The page links to FTC business guidance. This is the strongest trust angle against competitors who still claim "antibacterial bamboo".
- Redirects: polo, sweatshirt and workwear legacy URLs now 301 to the pages that own those keywords, so no thin duplicate pages are built.

## Batch 6 (denim, swimwear, sports bra, puffer jacket, blazer)

Owner confirmed on 26 November 2026 that these are made in-house. Research notes:
- Denim: buyers care most about fit, wash development and shade consistency between lots. Many competitors list MOQ per style but hide that each wash is a separate run, so our page says it plainly. Laser and hand distressing are not claimed.
- Swimwear: key buyer worries are wet opacity, chlorine and salt resistance, lining and rubber swim elastic. Chlorine and UV claims are left to the mill's test report. Underwired tops are stated as not made (to confirm).
- Sports bra: support levels (light, medium, high), removable pads vs moulded cups, and band and strap design. Wired bras are stated as not made.
- Puffer jacket: down vs synthetic fill, quilting vs baffles, down leakage. Fill power and down sourcing paperwork come from the down supplier. Seam-sealed waterproof puffers are stated as not made.
- Blazer: fused vs half-canvas construction explained. Full canvas is stated as not offered.
- Socks, sweaters and knitwear-factory URLs now redirect to /products/. The denim-jacket URL now points to the denim page.

## Batch 8 (varsity, fleece, softshell, coat, leather jacket)

- Varsity: buyers are streetwear brands, schools and teams. Key choices are body fabric (melton wool, satin, fleece), sleeves (leather, PU or contrast wool), striped rib and chenille patches. Wool and leather jackets are usually dry clean only.
- Fleece: polar vs micro vs sherpa vs high-pile, anti-pill finish, quarter-zip for corporate wear. Recycled fleece claims rest on the mill's certificate.
- Softshell: 2-layer bonded vs 3-layer with membrane. Softshells are water repellent, not waterproof, and the page points heavy-rain buyers to the rain jacket page.
- Coat: melton, double-faced wool, gabardine. Fused vs canvassed fronts. The old /coat-manufacturer/ URL now has its own page again.
- Leather: cowhide, lambskin, goatskin, suede and PU. Hide sorting by shade is the key quality step. Old leather and faux leather clothing URLs now redirect here.

## Batch 9 (football kit, basketball uniform, cycling jersey, rugby shirt, baseball uniform)

- Team kits: competitors sell sublimated kits with names and numbers, often from 10 to 30 pieces per design. Our MOQ is per design, with names, numbers and sizes free to change inside it, so a club can cover several teams in one design. Roster checking is the key quality step.
- Cycling: race vs club vs gravel fit, chamois pads from specialist pad makers, silicone grippers, three rear pockets. Old /cycling-apparel-manufacturer/ now redirects here.
- Rugby: heavyweight cotton lifestyle shirts (twill collar, rubber buttons) vs polyester match jerseys.
- Baseball: full-button jerseys, double-knit pants, tackle twill vs sublimation, softball fits.
- Site structure: the header menu and home page now show 12 core products only. /products/ lists everything.

## Batch 10 (esports, cheer, dancewear, martial arts, fightwear)

- Esports: sublimation is the norm because designs use dark bases, gradients and many sponsors. Fabric should be soft and matte for long sessions on camera. Fan jerseys in the player design are a common add-on.
- Cheer: shell tops, bodyliners, pleated skirts, all-star crop tops, tackle twill and rhinestones. Squad sizing sessions before bulk.
- Dancewear: nylon-spandex for ballet, cotton-spandex for class, covered leg elastic, front lining. Old /dance-wear-manufacturer/ now redirects here.
- Martial arts: single, pearl, gold and double weave; federations set colour and fit rules, which the page mentions without naming any body.
- Fightwear: MMA vs boxing vs Muay Thai shorts, rash guards with silicone grippers.

## Batch 11 (golf, tennis, running, compression, base layers), 26 November 2026

- Golf: ranking factory pages lead with stretch polyester or nylon-spandex polos, embroidery and club/event buyers. UPF claims on competitor pages rest on fabric ratings, so ours say the rating comes from the mill. "Polo shirt manufacturer" stays on the T-shirt page; golf targets "golf polo shirts".
- Tennis: buyers ask about built-in bras, ball shorts, pleats and opacity of whites. "Pleated skirts" stays on the skirts page.
- Running: race organisers care most about delivery dates and size splits. Runners care about chafing, pockets and reflectives.
- Compression: sports compression only. Medical compression is regulated with measured pressure, so the page says plainly we do not make or label medical garments.
- Base layers: merino vs synthetic and weight choice dominate buyer questions. Merino shrinkage and holes are the common complaints.

## Batch 12 (ski wear, chef uniform, apron, lab coat, coverall), 26 November 2026

- Ski wear: buyer pages lead with waterproof ratings in mm (10,000 to 20,000 mm is the common ski range), taped seams, powder skirts and pit zips. Ratings come from the mill's hydrostatic head test, so our page says so. "Waterproof jacket manufacturer" and "seam-taped jackets" stay on the rain jacket page.
- Chef uniforms: 65/35 poly-cotton twill dominates retail listings, with mesh backs, sleeve thermometer pockets and double-breasted fronts. Oil-repellent finishes are mill claims. "Hospitality uniforms" stays on the uniform page.
- Aprons: buyers compare bib, cross-back and waist styles and fabrics (twill, canvas, denim). Home brands need retail labels and packing.
- Lab coats: poly-cotton twill, knee vs hip length, embroidered names. Protective lab coats are PPE, so the page says the buyer arranges garment testing.
- Coveralls: poly-cotton twill with reflective tape is the common spec. Hi-vis and flame-resistant certification of the finished garment is arranged by the buyer. Asked the owner to confirm (CLAUDE.md section 19).
