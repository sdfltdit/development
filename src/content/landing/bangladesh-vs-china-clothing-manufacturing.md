## Why buyers compare Bangladesh and China

China has been the world's largest clothing exporter for decades. Bangladesh grew into one of the largest by focusing on cotton knitwear and basics at scale. Many brands now use both, or move part of their range from one to the other.

This guide compares Bangladesh vs China clothing manufacturing on what matters to a buyer. We are a factory in Bangladesh, so we have tried to be fair about where China is the better choice.

## Bangladesh vs China clothing manufacturing side by side

| Factor | Bangladesh | China |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strongest products | Cotton tees, polos, hoodies, sweats, denim, woven shirts | Technical outerwear, synthetics, activewear, complex and fashion pieces |
| Fabric base | Strong in cotton knit fabric, imports much woven fabric and cotton fibre | Makes almost every fabric and trim |
| Typical buyer | Brands buying basics and core styles in volume | Brands needing range, speed and technical work |
| Labour cost | Lower | Higher |
| Trade access in the EU and UK | Duty-free as a least developed country, with transition after 2029 | Standard duty |
| Trade access in the US | Standard duty plus any extra tariffs in force | Standard duty plus extra tariffs, which have often been higher |
| Best fit | Price-led cotton programmes | Product-led technical programmes |

## Product strengths

### Where Bangladesh leads

Bangladesh is built around cotton knitwear. T-shirts, polos, hoodies, sweatshirts, joggers and kidswear are made in very large volumes. Many factories knit and dye their own fabric, which keeps cost and timing under control. Denim and woven shirts are also strong.

### Where China leads

China's advantage is depth. Almost any fabric, trim or process can be found close to the factory. That makes China strong for technical jackets, synthetic activewear, bonded and laminated fabrics, and fashion pieces with many components.

## China fabric supply and what it means

China fabric supply affects Bangladesh too. Bangladesh makes much of its own cotton knit fabric, but many woven fabrics, synthetics and some trims come from China. For those products, a Bangladesh factory must allow time for fabric to arrive. For cotton knitwear from local fabric, it does not.

## Bangladesh vs China garment cost

Bangladesh vs China garment cost depends on the product:

| Product | Usual result |
| --- | --- |
| Cotton tee, polo, hoodie | Bangladesh usually lower per piece |
| Denim | Close, depends on wash and fabric |
| Woven cotton shirt | Close, depends on fabric source |
| Synthetic activewear | China often close or lower |
| Technical outerwear | China often lower, with more choice |

Always compare total landed cost: factory price plus freight, duty and fees. Duty can swing the result more than the factory price. Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) shows how.

## Trade access and duty

- **EU, UK, Canada, Australia and Japan.** Bangladesh has least developed country access to these markets. Its graduation date was moved to 24 November 2029, and markets such as the EU add transition periods after that.
- **United States.** Goods from both countries pay standard duty plus extra tariffs that differ by origin. US rates have changed several times since 2025 and new proposals are still appearing. Check the current rate for your product code before you price an order.

## China plus one

Many brands follow a China plus one approach. They keep technical or complex products in China and move cotton basics to Bangladesh or another country. It spreads risk from tariffs, cost changes and disruption without starting from scratch.

## Moving production from China

If you are moving production from China, plan it in stages:

1. **Start with simple, high-volume styles** that suit the new country's strengths.
2. **Send your existing tech packs and approved samples** so the new factory can match them.
3. **Check fabric routes.** Ask where each fabric comes from and how long it takes.
4. **Sample before you commit.** Compare quality side by side with your current goods.
5. **Keep complex styles where they are** until the new factory has proven itself.
