## Neighbours with different strengths

India and Bangladesh share a border and a long textile past, but their industries look different today. India has a huge home market, grows its own cotton and makes almost every kind of fabric. Bangladesh focused on clothing for export, above all cotton knitwear, and now ships more than most countries.

This guide compares Bangladesh vs India clothing manufacturing to help you place each product in the right country.

## India garment factories

India garment factories range from huge exporters to small expert units. They are strong in:

- printed and dyed cotton fabrics made close to the factory
- woven dresses, shirts and fashion pieces
- hand embroidery, beading and mirror work
- home textiles such as bed linen and towels

## Bangladesh's strengths

Bangladesh is built for scale on cotton knitwear. Many factories knit and dye their own fabric, which keeps basics low in price and quick to make. Denim, woven shirts and jackets are also big here.

## Trade deals in 2026

### The UK India trade deal

The UK-India Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026. UK duty on qualifying Indian textiles and clothing has been removed, provided goods meet the rules of origin. Bangladesh already had duty-free access to the UK as a least developed country, so the UK India trade deal removes a gap that used to favour Bangladesh.

### The EU-India agreement

The EU and India agreed a trade deal on 27 January 2026. Most duty on textiles and clothing goes when it starts. As of November 2026, it was due to be signed in December and to start in 2027.

### Bangladesh's position

Bangladesh is due to lose its least developed country status on 24 November 2029. The EU and others phase out the benefits slowly after that, so its duty-free access goes on for now.

### United States

Goods from both countries pay normal US duty plus extra tariffs set by country. These change often, and new plans keep coming. Ask your customs broker for the current rate.

## How to choose

1. **Split your range by product type.** Knit basics and woven fashion may belong in different places.
2. **Check duty in each market you sell to,** including rules of origin.
3. **Compare like-for-like samples.**
