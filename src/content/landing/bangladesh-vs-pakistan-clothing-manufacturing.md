## Two cotton-based industries

Pakistan and Bangladesh both built textile industries on cotton, but in different ways. Pakistan grows a large cotton crop and is strong in spinning, weaving, denim fabric and home textiles. Bangladesh imports most of its cotton fibre and focused on sewing clothing for export, becoming one of the largest clothing exporters in the world.

This guide compares the two for buyers deciding where to place each product.

## Pakistan garment factories

Pakistan garment factories are strongest where the country's own cotton gives an edge:

- **Denim.** Vertical mills spin, weave, dye and finish denim fabric.
- **Home textiles.** Towels, bed linen and kitchen textiles are major exports.
- **Knitwear.** Cotton tees and sweats.
- **Sportswear.** A long tradition in sports goods and team kits.

## Pakistan denim

Pakistan denim is known for vertical production, where one group controls fibre, fabric and finishing. That helps consistency in fabric. Bangladesh also has a large denim sector, with many factories sewing and washing denim from local and imported fabric. For denim, compare fit, wash and price on real samples. ## GSP Plus and EU access

GSP Plus is an EU scheme that cuts or removes duty for developing countries that follow a set of international conventions on human rights, labour, environment and governance. Pakistan has used GSP Plus since 2014.

A new EU regulation applies from 1 January 2027. Current beneficiaries, including Pakistan, keep GSP Plus during a transition to 31 December 2028, but must reapply under the new rules. The EU has said renewal is not automatic.

Bangladesh uses a different EU scheme for least developed countries. Its graduation was moved to 24 November 2029, with transition periods after that.

## United States

Goods from both countries pay standard US duty plus extra tariffs that depend on origin. These have changed several times, and new proposals are still appearing. Check the current rate for your product code with your customs broker.
