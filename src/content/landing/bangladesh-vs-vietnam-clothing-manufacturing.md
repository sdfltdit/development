## Two strong but different industries

Vietnam and Bangladesh both ship huge amounts of clothing, but they grew in different ways. Vietnam is known for sportswear, jackets and synthetic wear for big brands. Bangladesh is known for cotton knitwear and basics made at very large scale.

This guide compares the two so you can match each product to the right place.

## Bangladesh vs Vietnam clothing manufacturing side by side

| Factor | Bangladesh | Vietnam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strongest products | Tees, polos, hoodies, sweats, kidswear, denim, woven shirts | Sportswear, outerwear, synthetics, technical garments |
| Fabric | Strong local cotton knit fabric | Imports a large share of fabric |
| Labour cost | Lower | Higher |
| EU access | Least developed country preferences, with transition after 2029 | EU-Vietnam free trade agreement, with rules of origin |
| UK access | UK preferences for least developed countries | UK-Vietnam free trade agreement, with rules of origin |
| US access | Standard duty plus extra tariffs in force | Standard duty plus extra tariffs in force |
| Best fit | Price-led cotton programmes | Technical and performance programmes |

## Vietnam garment factories

Vietnam garment factories have long worked for big sportswear and outdoor brands. Many are set up for synthetic fabrics, bonding and taped seams. They often want high minimums and long-term work.

## Bangladesh's strengths

Bangladesh is at its best on cotton. Knit fabric is often made in the country, sometimes by the same group that sews it. That keeps cotton basics low in price and quick to make. Denim and woven shirts are also big here.

## Bangladesh vs Vietnam cost

| Product | Usual result |
| --- | --- |
| Cotton tee, hoodie, joggers | Bangladesh usually lower per piece |
| Cotton-based athleisure | Bangladesh usually lower |
| Synthetic performance wear | Vietnam often competitive |
| Down and technical jackets | Vietnam often stronger |

The Bangladesh vs Vietnam cost gap is clearest on cotton knitwear. On technical products, Vietnam's skill can be worth its higher labour cost.

## EVFTA rules of origin

The EU-Vietnam free trade agreement, often called EVFTA, cuts EU duty on Vietnamese clothing. To qualify, the EVFTA rules of origin generally require the fabric to be made in Vietnam or the EU, as well as the sewing. Because Vietnam imports much of its fabric, some garments do not qualify and pay standard duty.

Bangladesh's EU access uses looser rules, so a garment sewn from imported fabric can still qualify. That is one reason Bangladesh grew so fast in the EU.

## Sportswear sourcing

For sportswear sourcing, think about the fabric:

- **Synthetic and technical sportswear** (compression, bonded jackets, performance knits): Vietnam is often the first choice.
- **Cotton-based athleisure** (sweats, tees, joggers, hoodies): Bangladesh is a strong, lower-cost choice.

## US duty

Both countries pay normal US duty plus extra tariffs set by country. These have changed often since 2025, and new plans keep coming. Ask your customs broker for the current rate on your product code before you price.

## How to choose

1. **List your products by fabric.** Cotton or synthetic changes the answer.
2. **Check duty in your market,** including rules of origin.
3. **Compare like-for-like samples** from both countries.
4. **Compare landed cost,** not factory price. Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) shows how.
