## Why most launch budgets are wrong

Most founders price the garments and stop there. Then sample shipping, duty, labels and photos arrive one by one, and the budget is gone before launch day.

This guide breaks clothing brand startup costs into lines you can price one at a time. Where a number comes from our own price list, we show it. Where a cost depends on you, we show how to estimate it.

## Group 1: Development costs

Development is everything before bulk production.

| Line | What it covers | How to estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design | Sketches, colour choices, artwork | Your time, or a freelance designer's quote |
| Tech pack | Flats, measurements, materials list | Build it yourself or ask your factory |
| Pattern | The paper or digital pattern | Often included with sampling |
| Proto sample | First sample to check design and fit | Factory quote per style |
| Pre-production sample | Final sample in bulk fabric | Factory quote per style |
| Sample shipping | Courier to you and back | Courier quote by weight |

### What changes the price per piece

- **Fabric.** Heavier, special or custom fabric costs more.
- **Decoration.** More print colours or larger embroidery cost more.
- **Trims and labels.** Woven labels, metal hardware and branded trims add cost.
- **Quantity.** Price per piece falls as quantity rises. Our price list also shows prices at {{bulkQty}} {{moqUnit}}.

## Group 3: Getting goods to you

This is where budgets break. The factory price is not the price of goods in your warehouse.

| Line | What it covers | Who sets it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freight | Sea or air from the port to your country | Your freight forwarder |
| Insurance | Cover for loss or damage in transit | Your forwarder or insurer |
| Import duty | Tax on imported clothing | Your country's customs rules |
| Customs clearance | Paperwork and broker fees | Your customs broker |
| Local delivery | Port or airport to your door | Your forwarder or courier |

Our prices are {{incoterm}} {{port}}. That means freight, duty and clearance are yours to arrange and pay. Our guide to [Incoterms explained](/insights/incoterms-explained/) shows what each trade term covers.

### Work out landed cost per garment

Landed cost per garment is what each piece really costs you once it arrives:

1. Add up factory price, freight, insurance, duty, clearance and local delivery.
2. Divide by the number of pieces.

Compare landed cost per garment with your selling price, not the factory price. Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) has a worked example. A margin that looks healthy at the factory can shrink once duty and freight are added.

## Group 4: Launch costs

| Line | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Photography | Product photos and lifestyle images |
| Website | Store platform, theme, apps and domain |
| Marketing | Ads, content, samples sent to creators |
| Payment fees | Card and platform fees on every sale |
| Packaging | Mailer bags or boxes for your customers |
| Reserve | Cash for reorders, returns and surprises |

Keep a reserve. Your best seller will sell out first, and a fast reorder is how a new brand grows.

## Five ways to lower the cost to start a clothing line

1. **Launch with fewer styles.** Two or three strong styles beat ten weak ones.
2. **Share fabric across styles.** One fabric in your colours for a tee and a hoodie cuts sampling and fabric minimums.
3. **Use stock fabric.** Mill stock dyed to your colours is faster and cheaper than custom fabric.
4. **Keep decoration simple.** A one-colour print is included in our published prices.
5. **Ship by sea when you can.** Sea freight costs far less than air for most clothing orders.
