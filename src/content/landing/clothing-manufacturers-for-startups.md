## The mistakes we see first-time founders make

### Choosing on price alone

The cheapest quote often leaves out the sample, labels, packing or inspection. Compare quotes line by line, and ask what happens if bulk does not match the sample.

### Ordering too many colours

Every colour adds fabric dyeing and stock risk. Launch with two or three colours that you know will sell, then add more on the reorder.

### Skipping the pre-production sample

The proto sample checks the design. The pre-production sample checks the bulk fabric, trims and labels. Skipping it is how shade and size surprises happen.

### Paying the wrong account

Scammers copy factory websites and send fake bank details. We only ever ask you to pay the company account named on our proforma invoice, and our verify page lists every official contact.

## Budgeting your first run

Your landed cost per piece is more than the factory price. Plan for:

- **{{incoterm}} price per piece**, from your written quote
- **Sample fee**, shown on your quote
- **Freight**, sea or air, from {{port}} to your warehouse
- **Import duty and taxes** in your country. Ask your customs broker for the current rate
- **Labels, packaging and photography**

Our price index gives a dated starting point for a standard T-shirt, so you can sanity-check a budget before you ask for a quote.

## Why start with a factory and not a print-on-demand service

Print-on-demand needs no minimum, but you are limited to the blanks the service stocks and the margin is thin. A factory run gives you your own fit, fabric, colour and label from {{moq}} {{moqUnit}}, which is what makes a brand recognisable. If your volume is still below that, print-on-demand is the sensible first step and we will say so.
