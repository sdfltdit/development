## What happens on our production floor

Cut-and-sew is a chain of steps, and quality is decided at every one of them. Here is what happens to your order after the pre-production sample is approved.

### Pattern and grading

Your approved sample pattern is graded across the size set and checked against your measurement chart. Any tolerance you specify is written on the cutting ticket.

### Cutting

Bulk fabric is relaxed, spread and cut to a marker planned for minimum waste. Panels are numbered and bundled by size and shade so colours do not mix across one garment.

### Sewing

Bundles move through the sewing lines, where operators use the machines your construction needs, such as overlock, flatlock and cover-stitch. Line supervisors check measurements and stitching during the run, not only at the end.

### Decoration

Screen printing and embroidery happen in-house, either on cut panels or on finished garments, depending on the design.

### Finishing and packing

Garments are trimmed, pressed, labelled, folded and bagged to your packing instructions, then packed in cartons with a packing list.

### Final inspection

A sample from the finished lot is inspected to AQL {{aql}}. The report is shared with you before the goods leave the factory.

## Full package or CMT

Most brands choose **full package**: we source fabric and trims to your spec, and you receive finished, packed goods {{incoterm}} {{port}}. If you already have a fabric supplier, choose **CMT** and we charge for cutting, making, trimming and packing. Either way, the same inspection standard applies.

## Any fabric, knit or woven

We work with all fabric types. That means cotton jersey, fleece and French terry, but also polyester, blends, recycled fabrics and woven cloth. Send us your full collection in one brief. We'll quote every style together and tell you straight away if anything needs a special trim or finish.
