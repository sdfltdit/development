## Two kinds of sample

Every garment needs to be checked before bulk. Brands now have two ways to do that:

- **3D sample.** A digital garment built from the pattern and shown on a virtual body.
- **Physical sample.** The real garment, sewn in real or similar fabric.

It is not a choice of one or the other. Each answers different questions.

## 3D sample vs physical sample side by side

| | 3D sample | Physical sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to change | Fast, changes in hours | Slower, each round is a new sew |
| Shipping | None, shared as a file | Courier both ways |
| Design and proportions | Good | Good |
| Print and colour placement | Good | Good |
| True colour | Depends on the screen | Accurate under set light |
| Hand feel | Not possible | Accurate |
| Stretch and drape | Approximate | Accurate |
| Fit on a real body | Approximate | Accurate |
| Construction quality | Not shown | Shown |

## What 3D garment sampling does well

3D garment sampling is strongest early in development:

- testing silhouettes and proportions before cutting any fabric
- checking print size and position across sizes
- trying many colourways quickly
- planning a full range and showing it to buyers
- spotting obvious pattern problems, such as a sleeve that pulls

A virtual sample can save a physical round when the design is still moving.

## What only a physical sample shows

Some things only show in real fabric:

- **Hand feel.** How the fabric feels in the hand and on the skin.
- **True colour.** Screens vary, so colour must be checked under real light. Our guide to [what is a lab dip](/insights/lab-dip-explained/) explains why.
- **Stretch and recovery.** How a knit moves and springs back.
- **Fit on a person.** How it sits when someone walks, sits and lifts their arms.
- **Construction.** Seams, stitching, labels and trims.

## Fit samples and other sample types

Physical samples come in several types. Each has a job:

| Sample | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Proto sample | First check of design and pattern |
| Fit sample | Checks fit on a real body, sometimes in similar fabric |
| Size set | Checks grading on the smallest and largest sizes |
| Pre-production sample | Final check in bulk fabric, trims and labels |
| Top of production | Pulled from the first bulk pieces to confirm they match |

## How to combine them and cut sample rounds

A sensible mix keeps sample rounds low:

1. **Use 3D for design.** Settle shape, proportions and print placement digitally.
2. **Approve fabric and colour separately.** Use swatches and lab dips.
3. **Make a physical fit sample.** Check fit and feel on a real person.
4. **Correct and make a pre-production sample.** In bulk fabric, with labels.
5. **Check a size set** if your range is wide.

Each physical round you avoid saves a sample fee, courier costs and often a week or more.

## Limits of 3D sampling

3D models are only as good as their fabric data. Stretch knits, heavy fleece and fabrics with special finishes are hard to simulate exactly. Treat a 3D fit as a guide, never as approval for bulk.
