## Three ways to buy clothing overseas

When you source clothing from abroad, you can deal with a factory directly, buy through a trading company, or hire a buying agent. Each changes your price, your control and who you can hold to account.

This guide compares factory vs trading company vs buying agent so you can choose with open eyes.

## Factory vs trading company at a glance

| Point | Direct factory | Trading company | Buying agent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who they work for | Themselves, as maker | Themselves, as seller | You |
| Make the goods | Yes | No, use other factories | No, manage factories for you |
| How they earn | Factory price | Margin on top of factory price | Fee or commission |
| Price | Usually lowest | Higher | Factory price plus fee |
| Control and visibility | High | Low | Medium to high |
| Product range | Focused | Very wide | Wide |
| Best for | Brands with a clear product focus | Mixed small orders, many product types | Brands without time to manage suppliers |

## Buying direct from a factory

Buying direct from a factory means you talk to the people who cut and sew your clothes. Benefits include:

- no middle margin in the price
- faster answers on fabric, fit and timing
- clear responsibility for quality
- a direct relationship that improves over time

The trade-off is that you manage the factory yourself, and one factory rarely makes everything.

## Trading companies

A trading company sells you goods made in factories it knows. Good traders can combine products from several factories in one shipment and handle the follow-up.

The risks are a higher price, less control over where goods are made, and slower answers, because each question passes through a middle layer.

## Buying agents

A buying agent works for you. It finds factories, gets quotes, follows up samples, checks production and reports back. This suits brands that buy from several countries or cannot travel.

Agents are paid a fee or a commission. Check that the agent does not also take payments from factories, because that can bias which factory it picks.

## Sourcing agent fees

Sourcing agent fees are usually set in one of these ways:

| Model | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Commission | A percentage of the order value |
| Fixed fee | A set amount per order or per month |
| Hourly or project fee | For one-off searches or audits |

Ask for fees in writing, and ask what is included, such as quality checks, travel and sample handling.

## How to spot a trading company

Some trading companies present themselves as factories. To spot a trading company:

1. **Ask for recent photos and videos** of the cutting and sewing floor.
2. **Ask for the factory address** and check it on a map.
3. **Check the product range.** A real factory focuses on certain products.
4. **Ask technical questions** about machines, lines and capacity.
5. **Visit if you can,** or send an inspector.

Our guide on [avoiding clothing manufacturer scams](/insights/avoiding-clothing-manufacturer-scams/) covers more checks.

## Which should you choose?

| Your situation | Usual best fit |
| --- | --- |
| Clear product focus, want a lower price | Direct factory |
| Many product types in small quantities | Trading company |
| No time or team to manage suppliers | Buying agent |
| Buying from several countries | Buying agent |
