## Who moves your goods?

Once your clothing is made, it has to reach you. You can hire your own freight forwarder, let the factory arrange shipping, or handle it yourself. The choice affects cost, timing and how much you can see.

This guide compares the three routes, including doing it yourself.

## Freight forwarder vs factory shipping at a glance

| Point | Your own forwarder | Factory-arranged | Do it yourself |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Usual trade term | FOB | CIF, CFR or DDP | EXW or courier |
| Who books freight | Your forwarder | The factory | You |
| Price visibility | High | Low, built into the price | High |
| Control over timing | High | Medium | High, but more work |
| Paperwork for you | Low | Lowest | High |
| Best for | Most bulk orders | Buyers who want one price | Samples and small parcels |

## What a freight forwarder does

What a freight forwarder does is take the shipment off your hands. A good forwarder will:

- collect goods at the port or warehouse
- book space with shipping lines or airlines
- prepare transport documents
- arrange insurance if you ask
- clear customs through a broker
- deliver to your warehouse

You get one contact for the whole journey and a clear quote.

## Factory-arranged shipping

On CIF or CFR terms, the factory books freight to your port. On DDP, it also pays duty and delivers. This is simple, but you cannot see the freight cost, and the factory's forwarder works for the factory, not you.

Our guide to [Incoterms](/insights/incoterms-explained/) explains each term.

## DIY shipping

DIY shipping means booking carriers and customs yourself. It works well for samples and small parcels sent by courier. For bulk, it means dealing with shipping lines, documents and customs alone, which takes time and invites mistakes.

## Sea vs air freight

| Point | Sea freight | Air freight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Low per kilo | High per kilo |
| Speed | Weeks | Days |
| Best for | Bulk orders | Samples, urgent goods, small top-ups |

On sea vs air freight, plan production early so you can ship by sea. Using air because production ran late can wipe out your margin.

## LCL vs FCL

On LCL vs FCL, the choice depends on your volume:

- **LCL (less than container load).** Your cartons share a container with other shippers. Good for smaller orders, but handling fees add up and transit can be slower.
- **FCL (full container load).** You book a whole container. Cheaper per piece for large orders and less handling.

## How to choose

1. **For bulk orders,** use your own forwarder on FOB terms. Get two or three quotes.
2. **For samples,** use a courier account.
3. **If you want one simple price,** ask the factory for CIF or DDP, and compare it with a forwarder quote.
4. **Send your forwarder's details** to the factory early, so booking matches the ship date.
