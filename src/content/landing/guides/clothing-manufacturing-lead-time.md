## What lead time means

Clothing manufacturing lead time is the time between your first brief and the day your goods are ready to ship. Buyers often think only of the production weeks, but sampling, fabric and approvals usually take as long.

Knowing each stage helps you set a realistic launch date and spot where time can be saved.

## Stage by stage

| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief and quote | You send the brief, we price it | Within {{quoteHours}} hours |

## How long does clothing production take overall?

So how long does clothing production take? For a new style, a realistic plan from brief to goods in your warehouse is two to four months. The biggest swings come from:

- how many sample rounds you need
- how fast you reply with comments
- whether fabric is stock or custom
- the season you order in
- sea or air freight

A reorder of an approved style is usually much faster, because there is nothing new to sample.

## Build a time and action calendar

A time and action calendar, often called a T&A, is the shared plan for an order. It lists every step, who owns it and the date it must be done.

| Step | Owner | Due date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tech pack sent | Brand | |
| Quote confirmed | Factory | |
| Lab dips approved | Brand | |
| Proto sample sent | Factory | |
| Proto comments sent | Brand | |
| Pre-production sample approved | Brand | |
| Fabric in house | Factory | |
| Cutting starts | Factory | |
| Final inspection | Factory or inspector | |
| Ship date | Factory and forwarder | |
| In warehouse | Brand and forwarder | |

Work backwards from your launch date. If the dates do not fit, cut a style or move the launch, rather than squeezing production.

## What causes delays

| Cause | How to avoid it |
| --- | --- |
| Incomplete tech pack | Send measurements, materials and artwork up front |
| Slow approvals | Reply to samples within a few days |
| Many sample rounds | Give clear, specific comments each round |
| Custom fabric | Use stock fabric in your colours for first orders |
| Late trims or labels | Approve label and trim artwork with the sample |
| Peak season | Book production early |
| Changes after approval | Lock the design at pre-production sample |

## Peak season delays

Brands in many markets buy for the same seasons. That puts pressure on mills, dye houses, factories and shipping lines at the same time. Peak season delays usually come from:

- fabric mills with full order books
- longer queues at dye houses
- busy production lines
- tight space on ships

Plan launches for busy seasons earlier, and confirm fabric and production slots as soon as the sample is approved.

## Sea freight transit time

Sea freight transit time depends on the route, the port and the shipping line. Your freight forwarder gives the time for each booking. On top of the sea journey, allow for:

- container loading and port handling at origin
- customs clearance at destination
- delivery from port to your warehouse

Air freight is far faster but costs much more. Our guide to [Incoterms explained](/insights/incoterms-explained/) shows who arranges each part of the journey.

## Ways to shorten clothing manufacturing lead time

1. **Send a complete tech pack.** Our guide on [how to create a tech pack](/how-to-create-a-tech-pack/) shows what to include.
2. **Approve fast.** Every day a sample waits adds a day to the plan.
3. **Use stock fabric.** Custom fabric adds weeks.
4. **Keep trims simple** on a first order.
5. **Order the same style again.** Reorders skip most sampling.
