## Types of apparel manufacturers

"Clothing manufacturer" covers several very different businesses. Knowing which one you need saves weeks of emails.

| Type | What they do | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cut-and-sew factory | Makes garments from fabric on its own lines, to your design | Brands that want their own fit, fabric and colour | Minimum orders per style |
| Full-package manufacturer | Sources fabric and trims and delivers finished, packed goods | Brands without their own supply chain | Ask which steps are done in-house |
| CMT (cut, make, trim) | Sews fabric that you supply | Brands that already buy fabric | You carry fabric risk and logistics |
| Private label manufacturer | Makes to your design under your brand | Brands building their own label | Some sell existing styles as "private label" |
| Trading company or agent | Places your order with factories they work with | Buyers who want one contact for many categories | Margin on top. Factory may change |
| B2B marketplace seller | Lists products and custom services on a platform | Quick comparison of many suppliers | Badges are not the same as audits |
| Print-on-demand | Prints your artwork on stock blanks per order | Testing designs with no stock | Limited to available blanks. Thin margins |

If you want your own fabric, fit and label, you are looking for a **cut-and-sew or full-package private label manufacturer**.

## Where to find clothing manufacturers

These are the main places to look. Knowing where to find clothing manufacturers is only the start, so check each one before you send money.

- **Business directories** such as Clutch, Maker's Row and Thomasnet list manufacturers with services and locations.
- **B2B marketplaces** such as Global Sources and Alibaba let you compare many suppliers quickly. Verify each one independently.
- **Sourcing trade shows** let you meet factories and handle samples in person.
- **National garment trade associations** often list their member factories.
- **Referrals** from other founders, freight forwarders and pattern makers are often the most reliable source.

Where a factory is based shapes its price, lead time and what it does best. This is a general guide. Individual factories differ.

## How to find a clothing manufacturer you can trust: seven checks

The table at the top of this page lists the seven checks. In practice:

1. **Send everyone the same brief**, so the quotes are comparable.
2. **Reject vague quotes.** A good quote states the price per piece, sample fee, minimum per style, colour and size split, lead time and payment schedule.
3. **Ask who makes the goods.** If the answer is "our partner factories", you are dealing with a trading company.
4. **See the floor.** Visit if you can, or ask for recent photos of the production floor and buyer references.
5. **Pay for samples from your top two.** Judge fit, stitching and how they communicate.

## Questions to ask a clothing manufacturer before you commit

Send these questions to ask a clothing manufacturer to everyone on your shortlist, and compare the answers side by side.

- Which of these products do you make on your own lines, and which go to partners?
- What is your minimum per style, and how can it split across colours and sizes?
- What does the sample cost, how long does it take, and is the fee credited against bulk?
- Which inspection standard do you use, and can my inspector visit before shipment?
- What happens if bulk does not match the approved pre-production sample?
- Which company and bank account will appear on the proforma invoice?

## Red flags

- Prices far below every other quote, with no breakdown
- No street address, no way to see the factory, or reluctance to name the company
- Payment requested to a personal account, a payment app or a new account by email
- No sample before bulk, or pressure to pay quickly to "hold a slot"
- Certificates or badges offered instead of answers to direct questions

## How to choose apparel manufacturers by comparing quotes

How to choose apparel manufacturers comes down to comparing like with like. Put each quote into the same table: price per piece at your quantity, sample fee, minimum per style, lead time, payment schedule, inspection standard and shipping terms. Then add freight and import duty to compare **landed cost**, not factory price. The cheapest factory price often hides missing labels, packing or inspection.
