## How to price clothing in brief

Price too low and every sale loses money once discounts and returns arrive. Price too high and stock sits on the shelf. Learning how to price clothing well starts with one number: what each piece really costs you to land.

This guide gives a step-by-step method, the common multipliers brands use and the checks that keep your margin safe.

## Step 1: know your landed cost

Landed cost is everything it takes to get one finished piece into your warehouse.

| Cost | Example per piece, in any currency |
|---|---|
| Factory price | 6.00 |
| Freight and insurance | 0.60 |
| Import duty and broker fees | 0.90 |
| Inland delivery and handling | 0.50 |
| **Landed cost** | **8.00** |

These numbers are an example only, and work the same in dollars, pounds or euros. Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) shows each line in detail, and our [garment cost calculator](/tools/garment-cost-calculator/) adds them up for you.

## Step 2: set a wholesale price

The wholesale price is what a shop pays you. Many brands start at about two times landed cost.

**Wholesale price = landed cost × 2**

With an 8.00 landed cost, that gives a 16.00 wholesale price. Your gross margin on wholesale is 50%, which has to cover design, samples, marketing, staff and profit.

## Step 3: apply a retail markup

The retail markup is how much a shop adds on top of wholesale. Many apply keystone pricing, which simply doubles the wholesale price.

| Channel | Typical retail markup on wholesale | Example retail price |
|---|---|---|
| Keystone pricing | 2 times | 32.00 |
| Boutiques and department stores | 2.2 to 2.5 times | 35.00 to 40.00 |
| Your own website | Set your own, often matching store prices | 32.00 to 40.00 |

Keep your own retail price in line with the price your stockists charge. Undercutting them on your site damages wholesale relationships.

## A simple clothing pricing formula

For a direct-to-consumer brand with no wholesale, many use one clothing pricing formula:

**Retail price = landed cost × 4 to 6**

The higher multiplier pays for paid ads, free shipping, returns and payment fees. Then check the margin:

**Margin % = (retail price − landed cost) ÷ retail price × 100**

An 8.00 landed cost sold at 40.00 leaves an 80% gross margin before marketing and fees.

## Markup vs margin

Markup is measured on cost. Margin is measured on the selling price. The two are easy to mix up.

| Markup on cost | Margin on price |
|---|---|
| 50% | 33% |
| 100% | 50% |
| 150% | 60% |
| 300% | 75% |

## Check the price against reality

Before you print a price, test it.

- **Discounts:** if you plan sales at 30% off, check the margin at the sale price.
- **Returns:** online clothing returns are common, and each one costs postage and handling.
- **Fees:** marketplaces and payment providers take a share of every sale.
- **Competitors:** compare like-for-like fabric, fit and finish, not just the price tag.
- **Quantity:** larger orders lower the factory price, as our guide to [clothing manufacturing costs](/insights/manufacturing-costs/) explains.
