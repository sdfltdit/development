## How to scale a clothing brand when growth stalls

Launching a clothing brand is hard. Growing one brings new problems. Most brands do not stall for lack of demand. They stall because best sellers run out, slow styles tie up cash, or the factory cannot keep up.

This guide sets out the steps, with the planning that keeps growth steady.

## Step 1. Know your numbers

Before you grow, know what is working. Track these each week:

| Number | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Sales by style, colour and size | Shows best sellers and slow movers |
| Sell-through rate | How fast each style sells |
| Gross margin per style | Which styles make money |
| Return rate and reasons | Spots fit or quality problems |
| Customer cost to acquire | Shows if marketing pays back |

## Step 2. Double down on best sellers

The quickest growth usually comes from styles you already sell. Add colours, fill size gaps and keep them in stock. A proven style is less risky than a new one.

## Step 3. Reorder planning

Reorder planning means ordering again before stock runs out. Work back from your lead time:

1. **Find your lead time:** production plus shipping and customs.
2. **Find your weekly sales** for each style.
3. **Reorder point:** weekly sales times lead time in weeks, plus a buffer.
4. **Reorder when stock hits that point.**

Our guide to [clothing manufacturing lead time](/guides/clothing-manufacturing-lead-time/) shows how long each stage takes.

## Step 4. Inventory planning

Inventory planning keeps the right stock in the right sizes. Use real sales to set size ratios, not a standard curve. Buy less of slow colours. Clear old stock with bundles or sales so cash is not stuck in boxes.

## Step 5. Grow your range slowly

Add new styles in small test runs. Watch how they sell, then reorder the winners. Keep a core range that stays the same season to season, and add a few new pieces around it.

## Step 6. Manage cash flow for clothing brands

Cash flow for clothing brands is tight, because you pay for stock months before you sell it. Ways to ease it:

- staged payments to your factory
- pre-orders for new styles
- steady reorders instead of large one-off buys
- a cash buffer for marketing and surprises
- financing or stock loans once sales are steady

## Step 7. Add new sales channels

Once demand is steady, new channels can help:

| Channel | Pros | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketplaces | Big audience | Fees and price pressure |
| Wholesale | Bigger orders, new customers | Lower margin, strict delivery dates |
| Pop-ups and events | Direct feedback | Staff and stock costs |
| International shipping | New markets | Duty, returns and local rules |

Becoming a wholesale clothing brand means planning stock earlier and holding prices steady for retailers.

## Step 8. Build production with your factory

As you grow, share a forecast with your factory. A rough plan for the next few months helps them book capacity and buy fabric early. Ask about price breaks at higher quantities, and consider holding stock fabric for repeat styles.

## Common scaling mistakes

| Mistake | Better approach |
| --- | --- |
| Launching too many new styles | Grow best sellers first |
| Ordering by gut feel | Use weekly sales data |
| Running out of best sellers | Set reorder points |
| Spending all cash on stock | Keep money for marketing |
| Switching factories too often | Build one strong relationship |
