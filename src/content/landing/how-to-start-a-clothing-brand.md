## How to start a clothing brand with a clear plan

Many people want to start a clothing brand, but most stall between the idea and the first order. The brands that launch well usually do the same things: they pick a clear customer, keep the first collection small and work closely with one factory.

This guide explains how to start a clothing brand in ten steps, from first idea to launch day.

## Step 1. Find your clothing brand niche

A clothing brand niche is the specific group of people you serve and what makes your clothes right for them. "Comfortable clothes" is too broad. "Soft, plain basics for nurses on long shifts" is a niche.

To find yours:

- start with people you know well, such as a sport, job or community
- look for a gap, such as missing sizes, fits, styles or values
- check who already serves them and how you would differ
- talk to ten potential customers before you design anything

## Step 2. Know your customer and price point

Write down who your customer is, where they shop and what they pay now. Then choose a price point. Your price point decides your fabric, your factory and your margin.

A common rule is that your retail price should be about three to four times your landed cost per piece. That leaves room for marketing, returns and profit.

## Step 3. Write a clothing brand business plan

A clothing brand business plan does not need to be long. One or two pages that answer these questions are enough:

| Section | Questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Who buys, why and where? |
| Products | Which styles, colours and sizes? |
| Prices | Retail price and landed cost per piece |
| Budget | Samples, first run, photos, website, marketing |
| Sales | Online shop, marketplaces, wholesale or pop-ups? |
| Goals | Units to sell in the first six months |

Our guide to [clothing brand startup costs](/clothing-brand-startup-costs/) helps you fill in the budget.

## Step 4. Name the brand and protect it

Choose a name that is easy to say and spell. Check that the domain and social handles are free. Search the trademark register in your main markets, and consider filing a trademark before you spend on labels and packaging.

## Step 5. Design a small first collection

The right first collection size for most new brands is three to six styles. Pick a few core pieces your customer needs, such as a tee, a hoodie and joggers, in two or three colours.

A small range keeps costs down, makes sampling faster and shows you clearly what sells.

## Step 6. Turn designs into tech packs

A tech pack is the instruction file for the factory. It covers sketches, measurements, fabric, colours, trims, labels and packing. The more complete it is, the fewer sample rounds you need.

See our guide on [how to create a tech pack](/how-to-create-a-tech-pack/). If you do not have one yet, some factories can build it with you from a reference photo or sample.

## Step 7. Choose a manufacturer

Look for a factory that makes your product type, accepts your quantity and is open about prices and timelines. Ask for references, a sample and written quotes. Our guide to [how private label clothing works](/private-label-clothing-guide/) explains the main production models.

Key questions to ask:

- What is your minimum per style and colour?
- How long do samples and bulk take?
- What are your payment terms?
- How do you check quality?

## Step 8. Sample and approve

Expect one to three sample rounds:

1. **Proto sample** to check the design and construction.
2. **Fit sample** to check measurements on a real body.
3. **Pre-production sample** in the final fabric, colours and trims.

Do not skip fit checks. Fixing fit after bulk is made is expensive.

## Step 9. Produce your first run

Once the pre-production sample is approved, the factory buys fabric and starts bulk. Plan for checks during and after production, and agree how goods will ship. Order a modest quantity first, then reorder what sells.

## Step 10. Launch with a clothing brand launch checklist

Use a clothing brand launch checklist so nothing is missed:

- Approved samples and confirmed ship date
- Product photos and short videos
- Shop live with prices, size guide and stock
- Shipping rates and returns policy
- Email list and social posts ready
- Launch offer or event planned
- Customer service inbox set up
- Reorder plan for best sellers

## Common mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Better approach |
| --- | --- |
| Too many styles at launch | Start with three to six styles |
| Spending most of the budget on stock | Keep money for marketing |
| Skipping fit samples | Approve fit on a real person |
| Choosing a factory on price alone | Compare quality, terms and communication |
| No plan after launch | Track sales and reorder winners |
