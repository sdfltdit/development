## Who checks your clothes?

Every clothing order needs quality checks. The question is who does them. You can rely on the factory's own team, hire an independent inspector, or skip separate checks.

This guide compares the options, including what happens when no one checks.

## In-house QC vs third-party inspection at a glance

| Point | In-house QC | Third-party inspection | No inspection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who does it | Factory staff | Independent firm you hire | Nobody separately |
| When | Throughout production | Usually at the end, sometimes during | Not at all |
| Independence | Works for the factory | Works for you | None |
| Can fix problems early | Yes | Partly, if inline checks are booked | No |
| Cost to you | Included in price | Fee per inspection day | None upfront |
| Risk | Depends on the factory's honesty and skill | Low for what it checks | High |

## In-house QC

A strong factory QC team checks at every stage:

1. **Fabric on arrival,** for faults, width, weight and shade.
2. **Cut panels,** for size and shading.
3. **Inline checks** on the sewing line, so faults are fixed before they repeat.
4. **End-of-line checks** on finished garments.
5. **Final audit** before packing, often using AQL sampling.

In-house QC is the only check that can stop a problem early. Its weakness is that the team works for the factory.

## Third-party inspection

A third-party inspection company works for you. Its inspector visits the factory, draws a random sample using AQL tables, and checks measurements, workmanship, colour, labels and packing against your spec. You get a report with photos and a pass or fail.

Common types are:

| Type | When |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-production check | Before cutting, to confirm fabric and trims |
| During production check | Early in sewing, to catch problems in time |
| Final random inspection | When goods are finished and packed |

Our guide to [AQL inspection](/insights/aql-inspection-explained/) explains how sampling works.

## Inspection cost

Inspection cost is usually a fee per inspector per day, plus travel in some cases. One day often covers a final inspection for a modest order. Compare that with the cost of a full order with a sizing fault that you only find in your warehouse.

## Skipping inspection

Skipping inspection saves the fee, but the risk moves to you. Problems found after shipment are hard to fix, because:

- you have usually paid the balance already
- returns across borders are slow and costly
- reworking goods locally can cost more than the order

It can be acceptable for small repeat orders from a factory you know well. For first orders, new styles or large orders, an independent check is cheap insurance.

## How to combine them

Most brands get the best result by combining both:

1. **Choose a factory with strong in-house QC** and ask how it works.
2. **Agree the AQL level** and defect list before production.
3. **Book a third-party final inspection** for first orders and key styles.
4. **Add a during-production check** for large or complex orders.
5. **Agree in writing** what happens if a lot fails.
