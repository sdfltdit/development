## Circular fashion in brief

Most clothing still follows a straight line: make it, wear it and throw it away. Circular fashion bends that line into a loop. Garments last longer, get repaired and resold, and their materials come back as new fabric.

This guide covers the practical side for a brand working with a factory. It focuses on decisions you make at design and order stage, because that is where most of a garment's future is set.

## Why circular fashion matters now

For years, circular fashion was a choice. In the EU it is fast becoming a duty. The ban on large companies destroying unsold clothing applied from 19 July 2026. Extended producer responsibility will make producers pay for collecting and recycling textiles in every EU country by April 2028, with lower fees expected for durable, recyclable products.

The EU Strategy for Sustainable and Circular Textiles sets the direction. Clothing sold in the EU should be durable, repairable and recyclable, and made with respect for people and the environment.

## Make only what sells

The most circular garment is one that was never overproduced. Unsold stock is the biggest waste in fashion, and in the EU it can no longer simply be destroyed by large companies.

- **Order smaller runs** and reorder what sells, instead of betting on large quantities.
- **Test new styles** in a small run before committing to a full range.
- **Plan sizes from data,** so fewer odd sizes are left over.

Low minimums make this possible. Our [low MOQ clothing manufacturer](/low-moq-clothing-manufacturers/) page explains how small first runs work.

## Design for durability

Design for durability means a garment survives many washes and still looks good. Most of it can be specified and checked on the sample.

| Point | What to ask for |
|---|---|
| Fabric | Weight and yarn that resist pilling and holes |
| Colour | Good colour fastness, checked in the mill report |
| Seams | Strong stitches, the right stitch density and secure seam finishes |
| Stress points | Bar-tacks at pockets, plackets and belt loops |
| Trims | Zips, buttons and snaps rated for the garment's use |
| Fit | Shrinkage tested and allowed for, so the fit lasts |

Durable clothing also supports resale, because a garment that still looks good has a second life.

## Design for recycling

A garment that cannot be sorted cannot be recycled. Recyclers sort by fibre, so simple designs help.

- **Choose a mono-material** fabric, or a simple blend, where the design allows.
- **Limit mixed trims,** or make them easy to remove.
- **Avoid bonded layers** and coatings that block recycling unless the garment needs them.
- **Label fibre content clearly,** so sorters know what they are handling.

Recycled fabrics close the loop from the other side. Our [recycled clothing manufacturer](/recycled-clothing-manufacturer/) page covers recycled cotton, polyester and nylon, always backed by the mill's documents.

## Keep clothing in use

After the sale, a garment can stay in use through repair, resale and rental.

- **Repair:** supply spare buttons, offer repair guides or partner with a repair service.
- **Resale:** run a resale section, or work with a resale platform.
- **Rental:** suits occasion wear and fast-growing children's ranges.

Each of these works better when the garment was built to last in the first place.

## Take-back schemes and textile recycling

Take-back schemes collect used clothing from customers. Pieces in good condition are resold, and the rest go to textile recycling. Brands run them in store, by post or through partners.

Textile recycling today is mostly mechanical. Used clothing becomes rags, insulation or recycled fibre. Fibre-to-fibre recycling is growing, especially for cotton and polyester, but it needs clean, sorted material. EU textile EPR will fund more collection and sorting from 2028, as our guide to [EU textile EPR](/eu-epr-explained/) explains.

## Claims and honesty

Circular fashion is easy to overclaim. EU rules from 27 September 2026 ban generic environmental claims, such as "eco-friendly", without proof of recognised top performance. Use specific claims you can back, such as a stated share of recycled fibre with a certificate. Our guide to [EU clothing regulations 2026](/insights/eu-clothing-regulations-2026/) lists the rules.
