## CPSIA compliance for clothing in brief

The Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act of 2008, or CPSIA, sets the US rules for children's products. CPSIA compliance for clothing matters for any brand selling babywear, kidswear or school uniforms in the US. The rules cover testing, certificates, tracking information and chemical limits, plus separate flammability and drawstring rules.

This guide gives an overview for brands and importers. It is not legal advice. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) publishes the full rules, and a CPSC-accepted lab can confirm which tests apply to each product.

## Is your product a children's product?

A children's product is one designed or intended mainly for children aged 12 and under. The CPSC looks at how the product is marketed, sized, packaged and decorated. A size 6 dress with a cartoon print is clearly a children's product. An adult XS T-shirt usually is not.

Get this decision right first, because it decides which rules follow. If the answer is yes, the steps below apply.

## The core requirements

| Requirement | What it means for clothing |
|---|---|
| Third-party testing | Tests at a CPSC-accepted laboratory for every rule that applies |
| Children's Product Certificate | A written certificate, based on those tests, issued by the importer or US maker |
| Tracking labels | Permanent information on the garment and its packaging |
| Total lead content | No more than 100 ppm in accessible parts such as snaps, zips and buttons |
| Lead in paint and coatings | No more than 90 ppm in paint and surface coatings |
| Phthalates | Limits on plasticised parts of child care articles, such as sleepwear for children 3 and under |
| Flammability | 16 CFR 1610 for clothing textiles, plus 16 CFR 1615 and 1616 for children's sleepwear |

Many plain textiles are exempt from lead testing because the CPSC has found they do not contain lead above the limit. Trims, prints and metal parts are a different matter, so the lab will usually test them.

## The children's product certificate

The children's product certificate is a legal statement that the product meets every rule that applies. It must list the rules, the importer, the date and place of manufacture, the date and place of testing and the lab that did the tests. The importer issues it, even though the lab supplies the results.

Keep the certificate ready to show customs, the CPSC or a retailer. Retest on a regular schedule and whenever a material or supplier changes.

## Tracking labels

Tracking labels let a product be traced if there is ever a recall. For children's clothing, the label usually shows:

- The maker or private labeller's name.
- Where and when the garment was made.
- Batch, run or order details that link it to records.

The information must be permanent, so a printed care label or a woven label works well. It must also appear on the packaging where practicable. We print it on care labels in our own factory, to your wording.

## Children's sleepwear flammability

Children's sleepwear flammability rules are among the strictest US clothing rules. Sleepwear in sizes 0 to 6X must pass 16 CFR 1615, and sizes 7 to 14 must pass 16 CFR 1616. Most cotton pyjamas can only meet them if they are tight-fitting, which has its own exemption and labelling. Garments for infants of 9 months and under are also exempt.

Decide early whether a piece is sleepwear or loungewear. The answer changes the fabric, the fit and the labels.

## Drawstring rules

US drawstring rules treat certain cords as a hazard, because they can catch on playground equipment or doors.

- **Hood and neck drawstrings** are not allowed on children's upper outerwear in sizes 2T to 12.
- **Waist and bottom drawstrings** in sizes 2T to 16 must stay within length limits and be fixed so they cannot pull out.

Elastic, snaps or fixed cords usually solve the design. We agree the solution on the sample, before bulk.

## What your factory can do

Your factory cannot certify the product, but it can make compliance easier. Ask for these:

- Trims, threads and prints from suppliers that can share test reports.
- Samples for lab testing, taken from bulk materials.
- Tracking information printed on labels to your wording.
- Designs that avoid banned drawstrings and loose small parts.
- Records that link each order to its fabric and trim lots.
