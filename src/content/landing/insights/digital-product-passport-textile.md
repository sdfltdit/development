## The digital product passport for textiles in brief

A DPP is a digital record that travels with a product. Shoppers, repairers, recyclers and authorities reach it by scanning a data carrier such as a QR code. The digital product passport for textiles will hold data such as fibre content, origin, durability and recycled content.

Here we explain where the rules stand, what data is likely to be needed and how to work with your factory now. It is general information as of November 2026, not legal advice. The rules are still being written, so check the latest position before you plan.

## Where the rules come from

The passport is part of the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, known as ESPR. ESPR sets the framework, but it does not say what each product's passport must contain. That detail comes in a delegated act for each product group.

For clothing, that means nothing is mandatory until the textile delegated act is adopted and its transition period ends.

## The timeline so far

| Date | What happened or is expected |
|---|---|
| July 2024 | ESPR enters into force |
| April 2025 | ESPR working plan puts textiles in the first wave |
| May 2026 | EU research centre publishes a study proposing textile passport content |
| 20 July 2026 | The EU DPP registry goes live |
| 2027 (expected) | Textile delegated act adopted |
| 2028 to 2029 (expected) | Passports required, at least 18 months after the textile act |

The working plan dates are indicative and have slipped before. Treat 2027 as a planning date, not a deadline.

## What the DPP registry does

The DPP registry is a central EU database. It went live on 20 July 2026, ahead of any passport rules for clothing. Once the textile act applies, each passport will link to a unique product identifier recorded there.

You do not need to do anything with the registry yet. It matters later, when your passports go live.

## What the textile delegated act may require

The final list will come from the textile delegated act. Studies and drafts so far point to data such as:

- **Materials:** fibre composition, including recycled content and its source.
- **Origin:** where the garment was made and where key materials came from.
- **Durability:** results of tests such as pilling, colour fastness and seam strength.
- **Care and repair:** wash instructions and how to repair or recycle the garment.
- **Substances:** any substances of concern above set limits.
- **Identity:** a unique product identifier and the brand or importer's details.

Some of this already sits on your care label. Some sits in mill reports, test reports and factory records.

## Product data to collect now

The hardest part of a passport is not the QR code. It is gathering reliable product data from every supplier. Start with what you can get today:

1. Fibre composition for each fabric and main trim, from mill reports.
2. The factory, fabric mill and country of manufacture for each style.
3. Certificates for recycled or organic content, linked to each order.
4. Durability test results where you already test.
5. Care instructions that match the fabric tests.

Store it per style and per order, so it can feed a passport later. Our free [care label generator](/tools/wash-care-label-generator/) helps keep care wording consistent.

## How your factory can help

A factory cannot publish your passport, but it holds much of the data. Ask your factory to:

- Share mill reports and certificates with each order.
- Record which fabric and trim lots went into which order.
- Keep garment weights and fibre data per size.
- Use the same style codes as your own systems.

We supply this data with every order. The same records support your [EU textile EPR](/eu-epr-explained/) reports.
