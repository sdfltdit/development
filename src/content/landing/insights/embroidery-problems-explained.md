## Why embroidery goes wrong

Embroidery packs thousands of stitches into a small area of fabric. If the fabric, backing, design and machine settings do not suit each other, the result shows it straight away. The good news is that almost every fault can be caught on the first sample.

This guide covers the embroidery problems buyers see most, what causes them and how they are prevented.

## Common embroidery problems at a glance

| Problem | What you see | Usual cause | Usual fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Puckering | Fabric gathers around the design | Weak backing, tight hooping, high density | Firmer backing, correct hooping, lighter density |
| Poor registration | Outline misses the fill | Fabric moved during stitching | Better hooping and backing, adjusted digitising |
| Thread breaks | Snapped or frayed threads | Needle, tension or thread quality | New needle, lower tension, better thread |
| Gaps | Fabric shows between stitches | Density or underlay too light | More underlay or density |
| Backing show-through | Backing visible from the front | Wrong backing colour or excess not trimmed | Matching backing, careful trimming |
| Stiff patch | Design feels hard and heavy | Too many stitches or heavy backing | Lighter density, softer backing |

## Embroidery puckering

Embroidery puckering is the most common fault on knitwear. Jersey and fleece stretch, so the fabric pulls in as stitches are added. The fixes are:

- a cut-away stabiliser backing that stays in the garment
- hooping firmly without stretching the fabric
- a design digitised with the right density and underlay for the fabric
- pressing at the right heat after stitching

## Embroidery registration

Embroidery registration is how well separate parts of a design line up. When an outline drifts away from its fill, the fabric moved while the machine stitched. Firm hooping, the right backing and a digitising file that allows for push and pull keep it in line.

## Thread breaks

Thread breaks slow production and can leave visible joins. Common causes include:

1. **A worn or wrong-size needle** for the thread and fabric.
2. **Tension set too high**, so the thread snaps under strain.
3. **Poor-quality or old thread** that frays easily.
4. **Very dense areas** where the needle passes through many layers.

## Stabiliser backing

Stabiliser backing supports the fabric while it is stitched. Choosing the wrong one causes many embroidery problems.

| Backing | Best for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cut-away | Jersey, fleece, stretchy knits | Stays in the garment and stops stretching |
| Tear-away | Twill, canvas, stable wovens | Removed after stitching for a cleaner back |
| Water-soluble topping | Towelling, pile, velour | Stops stitches sinking into the pile |
| Soft or fusible backing | Garments worn next to skin | Feels softer against the body |

## How to check embroidery on a sample

1. **Front.** Check the design is sharp, outlines meet fills and there are no gaps.
2. **Back.** Check the backing is trimmed neatly and is not scratchy.
3. **Stretch.** Gently stretch the fabric around the design to spot puckering.
4. **Wash.** Wash the sample as the care label says, then check again.
5. **Position.** Measure the placement from fixed points such as the neck seam.
