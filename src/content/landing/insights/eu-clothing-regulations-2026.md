## EU clothing regulations 2026 at a glance

2026 was a busy year for EU clothing rules. Several laws agreed years ago started to apply, and more are close behind. This guide puts the EU clothing regulations 2026 brought in, and the ones that follow, in one place.

It is a general overview as of November 2026, not legal advice. Rules and dates can change, so confirm the details with an adviser or the official texts before you act.

## The timeline

| Date | Rule | Who it affects most |
|---|---|---|
| 19 July 2026 | Ban on destroying unsold clothing and footwear | Large companies selling in the EU |
| 12 August 2026 | Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation applies | Anyone placing packaging or packaged goods on the EU market |
| 27 September 2026 | Ban on generic green claims | Brands making environmental claims |
| 10 October 2026 | PFHxA restriction for clothing and accessories | Brands using repellent finishes |
| 17 June 2027 | National textile EPR laws due | Brands and importers in each EU country |
| 14 December 2027 | Forced labour ban applies | Every business selling in the EU |
| 17 April 2028 | Textile EPR schemes running everywhere | Producers of clothing and footwear |
| 2028 to 2029 (expected) | Digital product passport for textiles | Brands selling clothing in the EU |

## The destruction ban on unsold clothing

The destruction ban comes from the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation. From 19 July 2026, large companies may not destroy unsold apparel, clothing accessories or footwear. Medium-sized companies follow from 2030, and micro and small companies are exempt.

Destruction is still allowed in limited cases, such as safety risks or damaged goods. Otherwise, unsold stock must be sold, donated, repaired or prepared for reuse. Large companies must also report the unsold goods they discard, in a standard format from February 2027.

The simplest protection is to make less stock in the first place. Smaller, more frequent runs help, which is one reason brands use low minimums.

## PPWR packaging rules

The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, or PPWR, replaced the old packaging directive. Its PPWR packaging rules have applied directly in every EU country since 12 August 2026. For clothing, they cover polybags, hangers sold with garments, swing tags, boxes and e-commerce parcels.

Key points for brands:

- Packaging must be cut to what is needed, with limits on empty space.
- Recyclability and recycled content duties phase in from 2027 to 2040.
- Labelling and marking rules for packaging are being added in stages.

Ask your factory for packing that uses less material, such as smaller polybags or no inner box. Our [garment packing and export](/garment-packing-and-export/) page explains the options.

## Green claims from 27 September 2026

The Empowering Consumers Directive, (EU) 2024/825, applies from 27 September 2026. It bans generic environmental claims such as "eco-friendly" or "green" unless the brand can prove recognised top environmental performance. Specific, documented claims, such as a stated share of organic or recycled fibre, are the safer route.

Keep every claim tied to a certificate or test report. Our [sustainable clothing manufacturer](/sustainable-clothing-manufacturer/) page explains how we pass mill documents to brands.

## The PFHxA restriction

PFHxA is one of the PFAS chemicals, once common in water and stain-repellent finishes. Under the PFHxA restriction in REACH, clothing and accessories for the general public may not contain PFHxA and its salts at 25 ppb or more from 10 October 2026. The limit for related substances is 1,000 ppb.

Other textiles follow from 10 October 2027. Some countries, such as France and Denmark, have their own broader PFAS rules. Ask for PFAS-free finishes and a test report from the mill.

## Textile EPR and the digital product passport

Two bigger changes are still being put in place. Extended producer responsibility will make producers pay for collecting and recycling clothing in every EU country by April 2028. The digital product passport will add a data record to each garment, probably from 2028 or 2029.

Our guides to [EU textile EPR](/eu-epr-explained/) and the [digital product passport for textiles](/insights/digital-product-passport-textile/) cover both in depth.

## The forced labour regulation

The forced labour regulation, (EU) 2024/3015, applies from 14 December 2027. It bans products made with forced labour at any stage, from raw fibre to sewing, from the EU market. It applies to every business, whatever its size, and to imports, local goods and exports.

Brands should map their supply chain, including fabric and yarn, and keep records. A factory can help by sharing its own policies and the origin of its fabric.

## Labelling: what has not changed yet

Fibre content labels still follow the Textile Labelling Regulation, (EU) 1007/2011. A revision that could add digital labels and more product information was planned for 2026. As of mid-2026 the proposal had not been published, so check its status before you redesign labels.
