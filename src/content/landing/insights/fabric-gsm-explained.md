## What fabric GSM means

Fabric GSM stands for grams per square metre. Take one square metre of fabric, weigh it, and that number in grams is its GSM. A 180 GSM jersey weighs 180 grams per square metre.

Buyers use fabric GSM to compare fabrics quickly. It tells you how heavy a garment will feel, how much it will drape and, roughly, how warm it will be.

It does not tell you everything. Two fabrics at the same GSM can feel very different because of the yarn, the knit or weave, and the finish.

## GSM chart for common garments

This GSM chart shows typical ranges. Your brand may sit outside them on purpose.

| Garment | Fabric | Typical GSM | How it feels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light summer tee | Single jersey | 120 to 150 | Thin, airy, drapes close to the body |
| Standard tee | Single jersey | 160 to 190 | Everyday weight, not see-through in most colours |
| Heavyweight tee | Single jersey | 200 to 260 | Thick, structured, holds a boxy shape |
| Polo shirt | Piqué | 200 to 240 | Textured, holds the collar |
| Long sleeve tee | Single jersey | 160 to 220 | Same as tees, slightly heavier for cooler weather |
| Sweatshirt | Fleece or French terry | 280 to 340 | Warm, soft inside |
| Hoodie | Fleece | 300 to 400 | Heavier for winter and streetwear |
| Woven shirt | Poplin or oxford | 100 to 150 | Crisp and light |
| Chinos | Twill | 220 to 280 | Firm, holds a crease |
| Jeans | Denim | 340 to 470 | Heavy and stiff when new |

## GSM vs oz

In the United States, fabric weight is often given in ounces per square yard. Converting GSM vs oz is simple:

- **GSM to oz:** divide by about 33.9
- **oz to GSM:** multiply by about 33.9

| GSM | About oz per square yard |
| --- | --- |
| 140 | 4.1 |
| 180 | 5.3 |
| 220 | 6.5 |
| 260 | 7.7 |
| 300 | 8.8 |
| 340 | 10.0 |

Denim is the exception. Jeans are often described in ounces per square yard even in GSM markets, so a "12 oz denim" is about 407 GSM.

## How to measure GSM

You can check fabric GSM yourself with a cutter and a scale.

1. **Condition the fabric.** Leave it flat in a normal room for a few hours so moisture settles.
2. **Cut a sample.** Use a round GSM cutter that cuts exactly 100 square centimetres, or cut a careful 10 cm by 10 cm square.
3. **Weigh it.** Use a scale that reads to 0.01 g.
4. **Multiply by 100.** A 100 square centimetre sample is one hundredth of a square metre.
5. **Repeat.** Cut two or three samples from different places and take the average.

The full laboratory method for woven fabric is set out in ISO 3801. For buying decisions, a careful cutter test is usually enough.

## What GSM does not tell you

GSM is a useful start, but four other things change how a fabric feels and wears:

| Factor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Yarn | Combed and ring-spun yarns feel smoother than carded yarn at the same GSM |
| Knit or weave | A tight knit feels firmer than a loose one at the same weight |
| Fibre | Polyester feels lighter than cotton at the same GSM |
| Finish | Brushing, enzyme washing and softeners change the hand feel |

Always ask for a swatch. Our [cotton clothing manufacturer](/cotton-clothing-manufacturer/) page compares combed, ring-spun and carded cotton side by side.

## How to choose the right fabric GSM

Ask three questions:

1. **What season is it for?** Lighter for summer, heavier for winter.
2. **What shape do you want?** Lighter fabric drapes and clings. Heavier fabric holds a boxy or oversized shape.
3. **What price do you need?** Heavier fabric uses more yarn and costs more per piece.

## GSM tolerance on bulk orders

Fabric from a mill is never exactly the stated GSM. A tolerance of about plus or minus 5% is common. Agree the tolerance before bulk, and ask the factory to check GSM on every fabric lot when it arrives.

If the garment will be garment-dyed or washed, agree whether GSM is measured before or after washing. Washing can change it.
