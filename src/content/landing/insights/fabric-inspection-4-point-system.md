## 4-point fabric inspection in brief

A fault in the fabric becomes a fault in the garment. Catching it before cutting is far cheaper than finding it in a finished shirt. The 4-point system is the method most factories use to grade fabric rolls.

4-point fabric inspection gives each defect a score, adds the scores for a roll and scales them to a set area. Rolls under the agreed limit pass. Rolls over it go back to the mill or get cut around.

## How penalty points are given

The inspector runs each roll across a lit inspection table and marks every visible defect. Penalty points depend on the length of the defect.

| Defect size | Penalty points |
|---|---|
| Up to 3 inches (75 mm) | 1 |
| Over 3 and up to 6 inches (150 mm) | 2 |
| Over 6 and up to 9 inches (230 mm) | 3 |
| Over 9 inches (230 mm) | 4 |
| Hole 1 inch or less | 2 |
| Hole over 1 inch | 4 |

Two rules keep scores fair. No single linear yard can score more than 4 points, however many defects it holds. Only defects visible on the face of the fabric count.

## Points per 100 square yards

Rolls come in different lengths and widths, so raw points are scaled to the same area.

**Points per 100 square yards = total points × 3,600 ÷ (yards inspected × cuttable width in inches)**

For example, a roll 120 yards long and 58 inches wide scores 28 points. That gives 28 × 3,600 ÷ (120 × 58) = 14.5 points per 100 square yards. Under a 40-point limit, the roll passes.

The 3,600 converts square inches to square yards and scales to 100 square yards in one step. Mills that sell in metres use the same idea per 100 square metres.

## Fabric roll inspection: how many rolls

Inspecting every metre of every roll is rare. Common practice is:

- Check about 10% of the rolls in each lot.
- Include at least one roll from every colour and dye lot.
- Check more rolls, or the whole lot, if too many fail.

Fabric roll inspection also records width, length, weight and shade. A roll can pass on defects and still fail on shade or width.

## Pass marks buyers use

| Fabric or buyer | Typical limit |
|---|---|
| General woven and knit fabric | 40 points per 100 square yards |
| Knits for close-fitting garments | Often 20 to 30 points |
| Premium or light-coloured fabric | Often tighter, set by the buyer |
| Single roll | Many buyers also cap any single roll |

The right limit depends on the garment. A tight limit costs more fabric, because more rolls are rejected. A loose one risks more faults in cut panels.

## What the inspector looks for

Typical fabric defects include:

- **Holes, cuts and tears**, always scored at the top end.
- **Stains and oil marks** from knitting or weaving machines.
- **Slubs, knots and thick yarns** that show on the face.
- **Barre and needle lines** in knits, covered in our guide to [knitted fabric defects](/insights/knitting-defects-explained/).
- **Shading within the roll**, covered in our guide to [shade variation](/insights/shade-variation-explained/).
- **Missing ends or broken picks** in wovens.

## How it fits with other checks

4-point fabric inspection is one step in a chain. Lab tests check fibre, colour fastness and shrinkage. Cutting rooms mark and avoid small defects. Finished garments still pass an AQL inspection, as our guide to [AQL inspection](/insights/aql-inspection-explained/) explains.

Fabric defects also raise consumption, because cutters work around them. Our guide to [fabric consumption calculation](/insights/fabric-consumption-calculation/) covers how waste is planned.
