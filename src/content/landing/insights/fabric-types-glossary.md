## Why a fabric types glossary helps

Fabric is usually the largest part of a garment's cost, and it decides how the garment feels, fits and lasts. When you brief a factory, using the right fabric names saves time and avoids wrong samples.

This fabric types glossary covers the terms brands meet most often, grouped so they are easy to scan.

## Knit vs woven fabric

Every fabric starts as either a knit or a weave.

| Point | Knit | Woven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it is made | Loops of yarn linked together | Two sets of yarn crossing at right angles |
| Stretch | Natural stretch | Little stretch, unless elastane is added |
| Feel | Soft, flexible | Crisp, structured |
| Edges | Curl when cut | Fray when cut |
| Typical garments | Tees, polos, hoodies, joggers, leggings | Shirts, trousers, jeans, jackets, dresses |

The knit vs woven fabric choice also affects the factory. Knits and wovens use different machines, patterns and sewing methods.

## Natural vs synthetic fibres

Fabric is made from fibres. They fall into three groups.

| Group | Examples | Common traits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural | Cotton, linen, wool, silk, hemp | Breathable, can shrink or crease |
| Synthetic | Polyester, nylon, elastane | Strong, quick drying, holds shape |
| Cellulose | Viscose, modal, lyocell | Soft, smooth drape, made from wood pulp |

Natural vs synthetic fibres is rarely an either-or choice. Many fabrics are blends, such as cotton with polyester for less shrinkage, or cotton with elastane for stretch. Any claim about a fibre's performance should rest on the mill's test report.

## Common knit fabrics

| Fabric | What it is | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single jersey | Light, smooth knit with a flat face | T-shirts, tops |
| Rib | Knit with raised lines, very stretchy | Cuffs, collars, fitted tops |
| Interlock | Double knit, smooth on both sides | Kidswear, polos, base layers |
| Piqué | Knit with a small textured pattern | Polo shirts |
| Fleece | Knit brushed on the back for warmth | Hoodies, sweatshirts, joggers |
| French terry | Knit with loops on the back, not brushed | Lighter sweats, shorts |
| Waffle | Knit with a grid texture | Loungewear, thermals |

## Fabric weave types

The three basic fabric weave types are plain, twill and satin. Most woven fabrics are based on one of them.

| Fabric | Weave | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poplin | Plain, tight and smooth | Shirts, dresses |
| Oxford | Basket-style plain weave | Casual shirts |
| Canvas | Heavy plain weave | Bags, workwear |
| Twill | Diagonal lines | Chinos, workwear |
| Denim | Cotton twill, usually indigo | Jeans, jackets |
| Satin and sateen | Long floating yarns, smooth shine | Evening wear, linings, sleepwear |
| Corduroy | Woven with raised cords | Trousers, jackets |

## Other terms you will hear

- **GSM.** Grams per square metre, the usual measure of fabric weight. See our [fabric GSM guide](/insights/fabric-gsm-explained/).
- **Yarn count.** How fine the yarn is. Finer yarn gives a smoother fabric.
- **Combed cotton.** Cotton with short fibres removed for a softer, smoother feel.
- **Peached or brushed.** A finish that makes the surface soft.
- **Enzyme washed.** A wash that softens fabric and reduces fuzz.
- **Shrinkage.** How much a fabric shrinks after washing, set out in the mill's test report.

## Choosing fabric for clothing

When choosing fabric for clothing, work through these questions:

1. **What is the garment,** and how will it be worn?
2. **Does it need stretch?** If yes, start with a knit or add elastane.
3. **What weight feels right?** Light for summer, heavier for warmth.
4. **How will it be washed?** Check shrinkage and colour fastness on the test report.
5. **What price do you need?** Fabric choice moves cost more than any other factor.

Ask for swatches before sampling. Feeling the fabric is the quickest way to decide, and this fabric types glossary gives you the words to ask for it.
