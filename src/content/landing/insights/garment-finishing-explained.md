## What garment finishing covers

A garment can be perfectly sewn and still look wrong if it arrives creased, marked or packed badly. Garment finishing is the last stage before shipment, and it decides how your product looks when your customer opens the bag.

This guide walks through each finishing step and the faults to watch for.

## Garment finishing step by step

| Step | What happens | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thread trimming | Loose threads cut at seams, hems and buttonholes | The most common inspection fault |
| Spot cleaning | Chalk, oil or dust marks removed | Marks set in when pressed |
| Washing or special finish | Enzyme or other wash if the style needs it | Changes feel, colour and shrinkage |
| Pressing | Steam or press at the right heat | Sets seams and removes creases |
| Tags and labels | Swing tags, size stickers, barcodes | Ready for retail or your warehouse |
| Folding | Folded to a set size and shape | Looks the same in every bag |
| Bagging | Each piece bagged with your sticker | Protects the garment in transit |
| Cartons | Packed by size and colour, cartons marked | Fast receiving at your end |

Garment washes, such as enzyme or stone washes, are covered on our [garment washing service](/washing-service/) page.

## Thread trimming

Thread trimming sounds simple, but loose threads are among the most common faults in final inspections. Factories trim during sewing and again at the end of the line. Areas to check include hems, side seams, buttonholes and inside pockets.

## Pressing and folding

Pressing and folding set how a garment looks on arrival.

- **Pressing heat** must suit the fabric. Too hot leaves shine on synthetics and dark colours.
- **Steam** relaxes knits without flattening their texture.
- **Fold size** should match the bag, so garments do not crease further in transit.
- **Fold style** should be shown in your packing instructions, ideally with a photo.

## Garment packing

Garment packing is the last thing your warehouse sees. A clear packing list and carton marks make receiving fast.

1. **Bag each garment** with your sticker or barcode.
2. **Pack by your ratio,** such as one size per carton or a mixed size set.
3. **Mark every carton** with style, colour, size breakdown and carton number.
4. **Weigh and measure cartons** for the packing list and freight booking.
5. **Check carton strength** for the journey and your stacking.

## Finishing defects to look for

| Defect | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Loose threads | Hems, side seams, buttonholes, inside pockets |
| Marks and stains | Chalk, oil, glue or dust spots |
| Shine | Glossy patches from pressing too hot |
| Wrong tags | Size, barcode or price on the wrong garment |
| Poor folding | Uneven folds or garments too big for the bag |
| Mixed cartons | Sizes or colours not matching the carton mark |
