## Why landed cost matters

A factory price is only part of what a garment costs you. By the time it reaches your warehouse, freight, duty and fees have been added. If you set your selling price from the factory price alone, your margin can disappear.

Knowing how to calculate landed cost lets you price correctly, compare factories fairly and spot when a cheap quote is not cheap at all.

## The landed cost formula

The landed cost formula is simple:

> **Landed cost per piece = (product cost + freight + insurance + duty and taxes + clearance + local delivery) ÷ number of pieces**

Each part is explained below.

## The parts of landed cost

### Product cost

The factory price per piece times the quantity. Check which trade term the price uses. Our prices are {{incoterm}} {{port}}, which means the factory pays everything up to loading at the port, and you pay from there.

Our guide to [Incoterms explained](/insights/incoterms-explained/) shows what each trade term includes.

### Freight

The cost of moving goods from the port of export to your country.

- **Sea freight** is priced by volume, either a share of a container or a full container. It is much cheaper per piece but slower.
- **Air freight** is priced by weight or size, whichever is greater. It is fast but costs far more.

To find your freight cost per unit, divide the freight quote for the whole shipment by the number of pieces.

### Insurance

Cover for loss or damage in transit, usually a small percentage of the goods' value plus freight. Your freight forwarder can arrange it.

### Import duty and taxes

Import duty on clothing depends on three things:

1. **The HS code** of each garment, which says what it is.
2. **The customs value**, usually the goods plus freight and insurance in many countries, or the goods alone in others.
3. **The country of origin**, because trade agreements and preference schemes can lower or remove duty.

Some countries also charge import VAT, GST or sales tax on top.

### Customs clearance

Customs brokers charge to prepare and file import paperwork. Ports and terminals add handling and document fees.

### Local delivery

Moving goods from the port or airport to your warehouse.

## HS codes for clothing

The Harmonized System, maintained by the WCO, the global body for customs, gives every type of product a code. Countries base their customs tariffs on it.

For clothing, two chapters matter most:

| Chapter | Covers | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 61 | Knitted or crocheted clothing | T-shirts, hoodies, sweatshirts, leggings, knit polos |
| 62 | Clothing not knitted or crocheted | Woven shirts, jeans, chinos, coats, woven dresses |

The full code also depends on fibre, gender and garment type. A cotton knit tee and a polyester knit tee can have different codes and duty rates. Ask your customs broker to confirm the code before you order, because the duty can change your margin.

## A worked example

Here is a simple example of how to calculate landed cost. The freight, duty and fee figures are placeholders to show the method. Use real quotes for your own shipment.

| Line | Shipment total |
| --- | --- |
| Product cost: 1,000 hoodies at the factory price | Factory price × 1,000 |
| Sea freight to your port | From your forwarder's quote |
| Insurance | From your forwarder or insurer |
| Import duty | Duty rate × customs value |
| Import tax, if not reclaimable | Tax rate × taxable value |
| Customs broker and port fees | From your broker's quote |
| Local delivery | From your courier or haulier |
| **Total** | **Sum of all lines** |
| **Landed cost per piece** | **Total ÷ 1,000** |

Put the total next to your planned selling price. The gap is your gross margin before marketing and running costs.

## Costs people forget

- **Exchange rate.** The rate on the day you pay, plus any bank margin
- **Bank fees.** Charges for sending payments or opening a letter of credit
- **Sample shipping.** Couriers for samples, both ways
- **Storage.** Port storage if goods are not collected on time
- **Returns and defects.** A small allowance for faulty or returned pieces

## How to lower landed cost

1. **Ship by sea** whenever your timeline allows.
2. **Fill cartons well.** Good folding and carton sizes cut freight volume.
3. **Combine styles** into one shipment.
4. **Check trade preferences.** Some origins pay lower duty in some markets. Ask your broker.
5. **Get the HS code right** before you order, not at the port.
