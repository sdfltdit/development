## How to negotiate with clothing manufacturers: think in trade-offs

Good negotiation with a factory is not a battle over one number. It is finding changes that save cost without hurting the product, and agreeing terms that work for both sides over many orders.

This guide shows how to reach a fair price while keeping quality where you need it.

## Know what drives the price

Before you negotiate clothing prices, understand what you are paying for:

| Cost part | Share of price | Room to move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fabric | Usually the largest | High, through fabric choice and weight |
| Labour | Set by sewing minutes | Medium, through simpler construction |
| Trims and labels | Small to medium | Medium, through standard trims |
| Decoration | Varies | High, through print colours and size |
| Overheads and margin | Fixed share | Low |

Ask the factory for a price breakdown. It shows where savings are possible.

## Compare like for like

Quotes from different factories are often not comparable. Before you compare, check that each one has:

- the same fabric type, weight and fibre
- the same trims, labels and packaging
- the same decoration
- the same trade term, such as FOB
- the same quantity per style
- the same quality checks

A quote that looks cheaper may use lighter fabric, skip inspection or exclude labels.

## Levers that work

### Quantity and price breaks by quantity

Price breaks by quantity happen because set-up costs are shared by more pieces. If you can commit to more pieces, ask where the next price break sits.

### Fabric choices

- Use **stock fabric** in your colours instead of custom fabric.
- **Share one fabric** across several styles to reach mill minimums.
- Ask about a **slightly lighter weight** if it still meets your standard.

### Decoration and trims

- Fewer **print colours** cut screen costs.
- **Standard trims** cost less than branded ones.
- Move from embroidery to print, or the reverse, depending on size.

### Timing

Factories have busy and quiet seasons. A flexible ship date that fills a quiet period can be worth a better price.

### Repeat orders

A clear reorder plan is one of the strongest levers. Factories value steady work over one-off orders.

## Payment terms negotiation

Payment terms negotiation is easier once trust exists. On a first order, expect standard terms. After a few smooth orders, you can ask for:

- a smaller deposit
- a longer period to pay the balance
- payment by letter of credit if your bank supports it

Our guide to [payment terms for clothing orders](/insights/payment-terms-lc-vs-tt/) explains the options.

## How to ask

1. **Share a realistic target price** with your quantity and spec.
2. **Ask what would need to change** to reach it.
3. **Choose the changes you accept,** such as fabric weight or trims.
4. **Confirm everything in writing** on a revised quote.

This approach turns the factory into a problem solver instead of a defender of its price.

## Negotiation mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it backfires |
| --- | --- |
| Pushing price with no change in spec | The factory cuts corners you cannot see |
| Comparing different specs | You choose the wrong factory |
| Changing the design after approval | Adds cost and delay, damages trust |
| Asking for discounts after production | Strains the relationship for future orders |
| Hiding your real quantity | The quote does not reflect the right price break |
| Ignoring landed cost | A low factory price can still cost more |

## Build a long-term relationship

Lower prices usually come over time. Clear briefs, fast approvals, on-time payments and steady reorders make you a buyer factories want to keep. That shows in pricing, priority in busy seasons and flexibility when you need it.
