## What Incoterms are

Incoterms are short trade terms, such as FOB or DDP, that answer three questions in any sale of goods:

- **Where** does the seller hand over the goods?
- **Who** pays for each part of the journey?
- **When** does the risk of loss or damage pass from seller to buyer?

The International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) first published them in 1936. The current edition, Incoterms 2020, came into force on 1 January 2020. The ICC has begun work on a revision, laying the groundwork for Incoterms 2030, but no newer edition exists yet. You may see "Incoterms 2025" or "Incoterms 2026" in courses or quotes. Those are not official editions.

## What Incoterms do not cover

Incoterms are often misunderstood. They do not set:

- the price of the goods
- payment terms, such as deposit and balance
- who owns the goods at each moment
- what happens if one side breaks the contract

Those go in your contract or proforma invoice. Incoterms sit alongside them.

## The eleven Incoterms 2020 rules

The rules split into two groups by transport mode.

### Rules for any mode of transport

| Rule | Name | Seller delivers | Risk passes to buyer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EXW | Ex Works | At the seller's premises, not loaded | When goods are made available at the premises |
| FCA | Free Carrier | To the buyer's carrier at a named place | When handed to the carrier |
| CPT | Carriage Paid To | To the carrier, with freight paid to destination | When handed to the first carrier |
| CIP | Carriage and Insurance Paid To | As CPT, plus insurance to destination | When handed to the first carrier |
| DAP | Delivered at Place | At the named destination, ready to unload | On arrival, before unloading |
| DPU | Delivered at Place Unloaded | At the named destination, unloaded | After unloading |
| DDP | Delivered Duty Paid | At destination, import duty paid | On arrival, before unloading |

### Rules for sea and inland waterway

| Rule | Name | Seller delivers | Risk passes to buyer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FAS | Free Alongside Ship | Alongside the ship at the port of export | When goods are alongside the ship |
| FOB | Free On Board | On board the ship at the port of export | When goods are on board |
| CFR | Cost and Freight | On board, with freight paid to the destination port | When goods are on board at export |
| CIF | Cost, Insurance and Freight | As CFR, plus minimum insurance | When goods are on board at export |

A key point in the C rules (CPT, CIP, CFR, CIF) is easy to miss. The seller pays freight to the destination, but risk passes to the buyer at the start of the main journey.

## FOB vs EXW

These are the two terms new brands most often compare.

| Step | EXW | FOB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loading at the factory | Buyer | Seller |
| Inland transport to the port | Buyer | Seller |
| Export clearance | Buyer | Seller |
| Loading on the ship | Buyer | Seller |
| Sea freight | Buyer | Buyer |
| Insurance | Buyer's choice | Buyer's choice |
| Import duty and clearance | Buyer | Buyer |

EXW looks cheaper on paper, but the buyer must handle export paperwork in the factory's country, which is hard from abroad. FOB is simpler for most overseas buyers, because the factory handles everything up to the ship.

## FOB vs CIF

| Step | FOB | CIF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to loading on the ship | Seller | Seller |
| Sea freight to destination port | Buyer | Seller |
| Insurance at sea | Buyer's choice | Seller, minimum cover |
| Risk at sea | Buyer | Buyer |
| Import duty and clearance | Buyer | Buyer |

CIF can look convenient, but you do not control the shipping line or the insurance level, and you still carry the risk at sea. Many buyers prefer FOB with their own freight forwarder, so they control cost and timing.

## DDP shipping

Under DDP shipping, the seller delivers to your door with import duty and clearance paid. The buyer pays one price and does nothing else.

It sounds ideal, but:

- the seller must register for and handle customs in your country
- the seller adds a margin for duty, freight and risk
- few factories offer it, and those that do often charge more

DDP suits small or one-off shipments where simplicity matters more than cost. For regular orders, most brands use FOB or FCA with a freight forwarder.

## FOB vs FCA for containers

FOB passes risk when goods are on board the ship. In practice, container cargo is usually handed to the carrier at a container terminal days before loading. The ICC notes that FCA often fits container shipments better, because risk then passes at the point the goods are actually handed over.

In clothing, FOB is still the term most factories quote. If you use FOB for containers, agree with your factory and forwarder where the handover really happens.

## How to choose the right Incoterms

Ask three questions:

1. **Do you have a freight forwarder?** If yes, FOB or FCA gives you control over freight cost and timing.
2. **Can you handle export clearance abroad?** If not, avoid EXW.
3. **Do you want one all-in price?** DDP or DAP can work, at a higher price.

Always write the rule, the named place and the edition, for example "FOB Chittagong, Incoterms 2020". The named place matters as much as the rule.

## Incoterms and landed cost

Whichever rule you choose, work out the landed cost per garment: factory price plus freight, insurance, duty and clearance, divided by the number of pieces. Our guide to [clothing brand startup costs](/clothing-brand-startup-costs/) has a worksheet for it.
