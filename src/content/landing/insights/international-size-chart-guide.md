## Why an international size chart matters

If you sell abroad, your customer may think in UK, US, EU or Australian sizes. A "medium" or a "size 10" means different things in each. A clear international size chart cuts returns and support questions.

This guide shows how the main systems compare and how to build a chart your customers trust.

## Women's sizes: UK vs US sizes and EU clothing sizes

Women's numbered sizes differ the most between markets. This size conversion table shows the usual pattern.

| UK | US | EU (FR/ES) | EU (DE/NL) | Australia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 2 | 34 | 32 | 6 |
| 8 | 4 | 36 | 34 | 8 |
| 10 | 6 | 38 | 36 | 10 |
| 12 | 8 | 40 | 38 | 12 |
| 14 | 10 | 42 | 40 | 14 |
| 16 | 12 | 44 | 42 | 16 |
| 18 | 14 | 46 | 44 | 18 |

Two rules of thumb:

- **UK vs US sizes:** US is usually four lower than UK.
- **EU clothing sizes:** France and Spain usually add 28 to the UK size. Germany and the Netherlands often add 26.

Italian sizes run differently again. Treat every conversion as a guide, not a promise.

## Men's sizes

Men's tops are usually sold in letter sizes or by chest measurement. Letter sizes vary a lot between brands, so this is only an example of a common range for a regular-fit tee.

| Size | Chest, body (cm) | Chest, body (inches) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S | 86 to 91 | 34 to 36 |
| M | 96 to 101 | 38 to 40 |
| L | 106 to 111 | 42 to 44 |
| XL | 116 to 121 | 46 to 48 |
| XXL | 126 to 131 | 50 to 52 |

Men's trousers are often sized by waist and leg in inches, such as 32/32, in the UK, US and Australia. In much of Europe, numbered sizes are used instead.

## The European standard for size labels

The European standard EN 13402 describes clothing size by the body measurements of the person it fits, in centimetres. A label might show chest, waist and height. Some brands use its pictogram on labels. Older ISO size designation standards have been revised and replaced by parts of ISO 8559, which follows a similar approach.

Even if you do not follow the standard closely, its idea is the right one: describe sizes by real measurements.

## How to build a size chart

1. **Pick your customer.** Age, build and the fit you want: slim, regular or oversized.
2. **Choose a base size.** Usually a medium.
3. **Measure a garment that fits.** Take every point of measure.
4. **Grade the other sizes.** Our guide to [garment grading](/insights/garment-grading-explained/) shows typical size increments.
5. **Decide body or garment measurements.** Or show both.
6. **Add conversions.** Show UK, US and EU equivalents if you sell in those markets.
7. **Test it.** Have a few people of different sizes try the samples and pick a size from the chart.

### What to show on your website

| Column | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Your size | The label on the garment |
| Body chest, waist, hip | Helps shoppers choose |
| Garment length and width | Helps compare with clothes they own |
| UK, US, EU equivalent | Helps shoppers who think in one system |
| Fit note | Slim, regular or oversized |

Show centimetres and inches. Shoppers in the US and UK often think in inches.

## Size labels inside the garment

Your size label should match your chart exactly. If you sell in several markets, some brands print a short international size chart on the label, such as "M / UK 12 / US 8 / EU 40". Check the labelling rules for each market. Our [custom garment labels](/garment-labels/) page explains label types and placement.

## Children's sizes

Children's clothing is usually sized by age or by height in centimetres. Height is more reliable, because children of the same age vary widely. Many brands show both, such as "7 to 8 years, 122 to 128 cm".

| Size | Typical height | Common age |
|---|---|---|
| Baby 0 to 3 months | Up to 62 cm | Newborn to 3 months |
| Baby 6 to 12 months | 68 to 80 cm | 6 to 12 months |
| Toddler 2T to 3T | 86 to 98 cm | 2 to 3 years |
| Kids 4 to 6 | 104 to 116 cm | 4 to 6 years |
| Kids 7 to 10 | 122 to 140 cm | 7 to 10 years |
| Kids 11 to 14 | 146 to 164 cm | 11 to 14 years |

Height ranges differ a little between brands and countries, so treat these as a starting point. Children grow in steps of about 6 cm a size, which is why most kidswear charts move in 6 cm height bands.

## Unisex sizing

Unisex clothing is usually built on a men's block, with a straighter body and longer length. Women often size down one size. Show chest, length and sleeve in centimetres on your chart, so every shopper can compare against a garment they already own. A short note such as "women's fit: size down" cuts returns.

## Tall and petite ranges

Tall and petite ranges keep the width of a regular size but change the lengths. A tall range typically adds about 5 to 8 cm to body, sleeve and leg length. A petite range takes about the same off and raises the waist and armhole slightly. Each range needs its own grade rules, as our guide to [garment grading](/insights/garment-grading-explained/) explains.
