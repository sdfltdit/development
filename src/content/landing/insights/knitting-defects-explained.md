## Why knitted fabric needs checking

Knitted fabric is made of thousands of interlocking loops. One broken needle, one odd yarn lot or one uneven dye bath can leave a fault along a whole roll. Once that fabric is cut, every panel carries the fault.

This guide covers the knitted fabric defects buyers see most and how they are caught before cutting.

## Common knitted fabric defects

| Defect | What it looks like | Usual cause | Where it starts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Holes | Small breaks in the fabric | Broken needle, yarn fault, tension | Knitting |
| Needle lines | Vertical line along the roll | Bent or worn needle | Knitting |
| Barre | Horizontal stripes of different shade | Mixed yarn lots, uneven tension | Yarn and knitting |
| Spirality | Side seams twist after washing | Loop angle, yarn twist, finishing | Knitting and finishing |
| Slubs and neps | Thick spots or small knots | Yarn quality | Spinning |
| Dye marks | Streaks, patches or spots | Uneven dyeing or rinsing | Dyeing |
| Pilling | Small balls on the surface | Fibre type, yarn, finish | Yarn and finishing |

## Barre in knitted fabric

Barre in knitted fabric shows as faint horizontal stripes, most visible in solid dark colours under strong light. It usually comes from:

- yarn from different lots that takes dye differently
- uneven tension on the knitting machine
- a mix of yarn counts in one roll

The fix starts at the mill: one yarn lot per order and steady machine settings.

## Needle lines

Needle lines run vertically along the whole roll. Because the fault repeats, a single bad needle can spoil a large amount of fabric. Mills replace needles on a schedule, and fabric inspection catches lines before cutting.

## Spirality

Spirality is the twisting of knitted fabric after washing, which pulls T-shirt side seams round to the front. It is affected by yarn twist, knitting machine type and finishing. Controls include:

1. **Mill finishing** that sets the fabric before it is shipped.
2. **A wash test** on the fabric before cutting.
3. **Agreed limits** for twist after washing.
4. **Cutting on grain**, so panels are not cut at an angle.

## Fabric inspection before cutting

Fabric inspection before cutting is the cheapest point to catch a fault. A typical check covers:

| Check | What is looked at |
| --- | --- |
| Visual | Holes, lines, barre, slubs, marks, on a lit inspection table |
| Weight | GSM against the agreed weight and tolerance |
| Width | Usable width against the spec |
| Shade | Rolls against the approved standard and each other |
| Shrinkage | A wash test for length and width |
| Twist | Spirality after the wash test |

Faults are marked so the cutter can work around them, or rolls are rejected if they fail the agreed limit. Most factories score the visual check with the 4-point system, as our guide to [4-point fabric inspection](/insights/fabric-inspection-4-point-system/) explains.
