## Why a garment costs what it does

When two factories quote very different prices for what looks like the same T-shirt, the gap almost always comes from what is inside the price. Knowing what makes up clothing manufacturing costs helps you compare quotes and decide where to save.

## The cost breakdown of a garment

Every factory price is built from the same parts:

| Part | What it covers | What moves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fabric | Main fabric and any lining or rib | Fibre, weight, width, finish, consumption |
| CM cost | Cutting and sewing labour | Sewing minutes, construction, skill needed |
| Trims | Zips, buttons, cords, elastic, thread | Type, brand, quantity |
| Labels and packing | Brand label, care label, tags, polybags, cartons | Material, number of labels |
| Decoration | Print, embroidery, patches | Size, colours, stitch count, method |
| Wash or dye | Enzyme wash, garment dye, special finishes | Process and time |
| Checks | Inline and final inspection | Standard and number of checks |
| Overheads and margin | Factory costs and profit | Fairly fixed share |

## Fabric cost per garment

Fabric cost per garment is usually the biggest single line. It depends on three things:

1. **Fabric price** per kilo or metre, set by fibre, weight and finish.
2. **Consumption,** which is how much fabric one garment uses. Bigger sizes and oversized fits use more.
3. **Waste allowance** for cutting, shrinkage and testing.

Our guide to [fabric consumption calculation](/insights/fabric-consumption-calculation/) shows how consumption is worked out, and the free [fabric consumption calculator](/tools/fabric-consumption-calculator/) runs the numbers. Heavier fabric, special fibres and custom colours all raise the fabric line. Our [fabric types glossary](/insights/fabric-types-glossary/) explains the common options.

## CM cost

CM cost means cut and make. It is set by how many minutes the style takes to sew. A basic tee needs few operations. A lined jacket with pockets, zips and taped seams needs many more, so its CM cost is higher.

Details that add sewing minutes include:

- extra panels and seams
- pockets, plackets and flaps
- linings and facings
- topstitching and bar tacks
- small or complex parts

## Trims, labels and decoration

Trims and labels are small per piece but add up. Branded zips and custom hardware cost more than standard ones. Each extra label is another cost.

Decoration varies the most. Screen print price depends on colours and size. Embroidery depends on stitch count. Some techniques, such as puff print or appliqué, add both material and time.

## How quantity changes clothing manufacturing costs

Quantity has a big effect because some costs are fixed per order:

- making markers and setting up the sewing line
- print screens and embroidery files
- minimum fabric and trim orders from mills

## Ways to lower production costs

To lower production costs without hurting what customers notice:

1. **Raise quantity per style** by cutting the number of styles or colours.
2. **Use stock fabric** instead of custom-dyed fabric where you can.
3. **Share one fabric** across several styles.
4. **Cut print colours** or reduce embroidery size.
5. **Choose standard trims** where the brand is not visible.
6. **Simplify hidden details,** such as inside finishes, only where quality allows.
7. **Plan ahead** to avoid rush charges and air freight.

## Factory price vs landed cost

The factory price is not your full cost. On {{incoterm}} terms, you add freight, insurance, duty, import tax and clearance. Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) shows how to add it all up.
