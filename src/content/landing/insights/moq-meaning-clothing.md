## What does MOQ mean?

What does MOQ mean when a factory puts it on a quote? MOQ stands for **minimum order quantity**. It is the smallest number of pieces the factory will make for you in one order.

Below the MOQ, the order costs the factory more to set up than it earns. Above it, the work makes sense for both sides.

## Why factories set an MOQ

An MOQ is not a sales trick. It comes from real costs that do not shrink with smaller orders.

| Cost | Why it sets a minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Fabric | Mills sell fabric by minimum lengths, and dye in minimum batch sizes |
| Dyeing | A dye batch for one colour costs much the same for few metres or many |
| Cutting | Laying and cutting fabric takes setup time per style |
| Sewing line | A line must be set up and balanced for each new style |
| Printing | Each print colour needs a screen, made once |
| Trims and labels | Label and trim makers also have minimums |

When these setup costs are shared over more pieces, the price per piece falls. That is why price lists show lower prices at higher quantities.

## MOQ per style vs MOQ per colour

How the MOQ applies matters as much as the number.

| Type | What it means | Effect on your order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MOQ per order | The whole order must reach the minimum | Most flexible |
| MOQ per style | Each style must reach it, shared across colours and sizes | Flexible within a style |
| MOQ per colour | Each colour of each style must reach it | Multiplies quickly |
| MOQ per fabric | Each fabric must reach the mill's minimum | Can apply to small fabric runs |

### An example

Say a factory's minimum is a fixed number of pieces, and you want one hoodie in three colours.

- With an **MOQ per style**, the three colours share the minimum.
- With an **MOQ per colour**, each colour must reach the minimum alone, so you need three times as many pieces.

Always ask the factory to state on the quote whether its minimum is per order, per style, per colour or per fabric.

## How sizes fit into MOQ

Sizes almost always share the minimum. You choose the size split, such as more mediums and larges than extra smalls. Some factories ask for a minimum per size so very small runs of one size are avoided. Ask before you build your size ratio.

## MOQ vs sample order

| | Sample order | MOQ order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Check design, fit and quality | Stock to sell |
| Quantity | One or a few pieces | At least the minimum |
| Price | Sample price per style | Bulk price per piece |
| Timing | Before bulk | After sample approval |

Never skip the sample to save time. Fixing a mistake in a sample costs far less than fixing it in bulk.

## How to negotiate MOQ

Factories are more flexible when you lower their setup risk. Try these:

1. **Share fabric across styles.** A tee and a hoodie in the same jersey or fleece colour help meet fabric minimums.
2. **Use stock fabric.** Fabric a mill already makes in your colour avoids dye minimums.
3. **Keep trims standard.** Branded trims carry their own minimums.
4. **Fewer colours first.** Launch with two colours, add more on the reorder.
5. **Show a reorder plan.** Factories value repeat orders over one-off small runs.
6. **Accept a higher price per piece.** A small run can be possible if you accept the extra setup cost.

Be wary of very low minimums with no explanation. They often mean stock blanks or leftover fabric, not your own product.
