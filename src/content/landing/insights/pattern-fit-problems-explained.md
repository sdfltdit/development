## Fit problems are pattern clues

A garment that does not fit is giving you information. Where the fabric pulls, folds or stands away from the body tells the pattern maker which piece to change. Learning to read those signs makes fit sessions faster.

This guide explains the garment fit problems buyers see most and what each one means.

## Common garment fit problems

| Problem | Where it shows | What it usually means | Typical pattern fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drag lines from bust or chest | Front body | Too little width or length over the bust | Add width or a dart |
| Gaping neckline | Neck | Neck opening too wide or band too long | Reduce opening or shorten band |
| Tight armhole | Underarm | Armhole too small or too high | Lower or widen the armhole |
| Collar standing away | Back neck | Neck seam and collar lengths do not match | Balance the collar to the neck |
| Hem riding up at front | Front hem | Too little length over the chest or belly | Add front length |
| Pulling across the seat | Back trousers | Too little seat width or rise | Add width or lengthen back rise |
| Twisting legs | Trouser legs | Off-grain cutting or unbalanced legs | Check grain and seam lengths |

## Reading drag lines

Drag lines are diagonal folds in the fabric. They point towards the area that needs more room or length. For example, folds running from the armpit towards the bust point to too little width there. Folds that fan out from the crotch of trousers point to a rise problem.

Horizontal folds usually mean too much length in that area. Vertical folds usually mean too much width.

## Ease allowance

Ease allowance is the room built into a pattern beyond the body measurement. It is what lets you move, sit and breathe.

| Fit | Ease | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Close or compression | Little or none, fabric stretches instead | Leggings, base layers |
| Regular | Moderate | Tees, shirts, trousers |
| Relaxed | Generous | Hoodies, oversized tees |
| Oversized | Very generous | Streetwear, outerwear |

Stretch fabrics need less ease, and some need negative ease. That is why the same pattern cannot simply move from a woven to a knit.

## Fit sample review

A good fit sample review follows the same steps every time:

1. **Use the right model,** matching your base size measurements.
2. **Check standing still,** then sitting, reaching and walking.
3. **Measure the sample** at each point of measure against the spec.
4. **Photograph front, side and back,** with problem areas marked.
5. **Write comments as measurements,** such as "add 1.5 cm to front length".
6. **Approve or request a new round,** with every change listed.

## Pattern adjustments that keep grading right

Pattern adjustments made on the base size must carry through every size. After a fix, the grade rules are checked so smaller and larger sizes keep the same proportions. Our guide to [garment grading](/insights/garment-grading-explained/) explains how this works.
