## Why payment terms matter

Payment terms for clothing orders decide who carries the risk if something goes wrong. Pay everything upfront and you carry the risk. Pay nothing until delivery and the factory carries it. Most terms sit between the two.

This guide explains the two main options, telegraphic transfer and letter of credit, and how to pay safely with either.

## Telegraphic transfer (T/T)

A telegraphic transfer is a direct bank-to-bank payment, often called a wire transfer. It is the most common way to pay a clothing factory.

### How T/T usually works

Payments are split into stages tied to the order:

1. **Deposit.** Paid to start sampling or to book fabric and production.
2. **Progress payment.** Sometimes paid when bulk production starts.
3. **Balance.** Paid when goods are finished and inspected, before shipment.

The exact split is set on the proforma invoice. Common splits are 30% deposit and 70% balance, or three stages.

### Pros and cons of T/T

| Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- |
| Simple and fast | The buyer pays before receiving goods |
| Low bank fees | Relies on trust and checks |
| Easy for small orders | No bank checks documents for you |

Protect yourself with a final inspection before you pay the balance. Our guide to [AQL inspection](/insights/aql-inspection-explained/) explains how that works.

## Letter of credit (LC)

A letter of credit is a promise from your bank to pay the factory, as long as the factory presents documents that match the terms of the credit. Most LCs are issued under the ICC's Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, known as UCP 600.

### How a letter of credit works

1. **You agree terms** with the factory, including the documents required.
2. **Your bank issues the LC** to the factory's bank.
3. **The factory produces and ships** the goods.
4. **The factory presents documents**, such as the bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list and any inspection certificate.
5. **The banks check the documents** against the LC.
6. **Payment is made** if they match, at sight or after an agreed number of days.

### LC at sight and usance LC

- **LC at sight:** the bank pays as soon as matching documents arrive.
- **Usance LC:** the bank pays a set number of days after, giving the buyer time to sell the goods.

### Pros and cons of a letter of credit

| Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- |
| Bank checks documents before paying | Bank fees to open and amend |
| Factory knows it will be paid | Slower to set up |
| You can require an inspection certificate | Small document errors can delay payment |

An important point: banks check documents, not goods. An LC protects you only as well as the documents you ask for. Add an inspection certificate if quality matters to you.

## Documents against payment

Documents against payment, often called D/P, sits between T/T and an LC. The factory ships and sends the shipping documents through the banks. Your bank releases them only when you pay. Without the documents, you cannot collect the goods.

It costs less than an LC, but the factory carries more risk, so it is less common with new buyers.

## LC vs T/T: which to choose

| Question | T/T | Letter of credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order size | Small to large | Usually larger orders |
| Setup time | Days | Longer |
| Bank fees | Low | Higher |
| Protection for the buyer | Through checks and inspection | Through document checks |
| Protection for the factory | Through deposit | Through bank promise |
| Paperwork | Light | Heavy |

For a first order of a few styles, T/T with a deposit and a final inspection before the balance is usually simpler. An LC makes more sense on larger orders or where your bank or buyer requires it.

## How to pay a factory safely

Whatever terms you choose:

- Pay only the **company account named on the proforma invoice**
- Check that the company name matches the one you have been dealing with
- **Never accept new bank details by email** without calling a number you already trust
- Avoid paying personal accounts
- Keep a copy of every invoice and payment receipt
- Tie the balance to a passed final inspection

Payment fraud often starts with a fake email saying bank details have changed. A two-minute phone call stops it.

## How payment terms affect price

Terms change cost for both sides. A factory that waits longer to be paid carries more risk and finance cost, which can show in its price. Clear, reliable payment terms help build a relationship that leads to better prices over time.
