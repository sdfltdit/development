## How to choose between clothing printing methods

There is no single best method. The right one depends on four things:

1. **Your artwork.** A two-colour logo and a photo need different methods.
2. **Your fabric.** Cotton, polyester and blends take ink differently.
3. **Your quantity.** Some methods have high setup and low running cost. Others are the reverse.
4. **The feel you want.** Some prints feel soft, others thick and raised.

The table below compares the main clothing printing methods on those points.

## The main methods side by side

| Method | Best artwork | Fabrics | Feel | Durability | Setup cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Screen printing | Solid colours, up to about eight | Cotton, blends, some polyester | Soft to thick | High | Per colour, per design |
| Water-based screen | Solid colours on light fabric | Cotton | Very soft | High | Per colour |
| Discharge | Solid colours on dark fabric | Cotton | Very soft | High | Per colour |
| Puff | Bold logos | Cotton, fleece | Raised and spongy | Medium to high | Per colour |
| DTF | Full colour, fine detail | Most fabrics | Thin film | Medium to high | Low |
| DTG | Full colour on light fabric | Cotton | Soft | Medium | Very low |
| Sublimation | Full colour, edge to edge | White or light polyester | None | Very high | Low |
| Heat transfer vinyl | Names, numbers, simple shapes | Most fabrics | Smooth film | Medium | Low |

## Screen printing

Ink is pushed through a fine mesh screen onto the garment, one colour at a time. Each colour needs its own screen.

**Good for:** brand logos, merch, and anything printed in bulk.
**Watch for:** setup cost on small runs and many colours.

Screen printing gives strong, lasting colour and the lowest price per piece on larger orders. With water-based or discharge ink, it can feel almost like part of the fabric.

## DTF (direct to film)

The design is printed onto a film with a white base, coated with adhesive powder and heat-pressed onto the garment.

**Good for:** full-colour logos, small runs and many designs.
**Watch for:** a thin film you can feel, and lower stretch than screen print on very stretchy fabric.

## DTG (direct to garment)

A printer sprays ink straight into the fabric, like an office printer on cloth.

**Good for:** photos and gradients on light cotton in small numbers.
**Watch for:** slower printing, and faster fading than screen print, especially on dark garments.

## Sublimation

Dye is printed onto paper, then turned into gas with heat and pressure so it bonds into polyester fibres.

**Good for:** all-over prints on sportswear, team kits and swimwear.
**Watch for:** it only works on white or light polyester. It cannot print on cotton or cover dark fabric.

## Effects: puff, high-density and discharge

These are variations of screen printing:

- **Puff** uses ink that rises with heat for a raised, soft feel.
- **High-density** builds thick, sharp layers that stand up from the fabric.
- **Discharge** removes the fabric's dye and replaces it with colour, so prints on dark cotton feel soft.

## Screen printing vs DTF

| | Screen printing | DTF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colours | Best with up to eight solid colours | Unlimited, including gradients |
| Setup | Screens per colour | Almost none |
| Price on bulk | Lowest | Higher per piece |
| Price on small runs | Higher, because of setup | Lower |
| Feel | Soft to thick, depending on ink | Thin film |
| Lifespan | Very long when cured well | Long, with care |

For a two-colour logo on several hundred tees, screen printing usually wins. For a full-colour illustration in small numbers, DTF usually wins.

## DTG vs screen printing

DTG is ideal when you need photos or many colours in very small numbers. Screen printing is better once quantities rise and when the print must survive many washes. Most brands that start with DTG move to screen printing for best sellers.

## The best printing method for t-shirts

For most brand tees, screen printing is the best printing method for t-shirts. It balances cost, feel and life. Choose:

- **plastisol** for bright, opaque colour
- **water-based** for a soft print on light tees
- **discharge** for a soft print on dark cotton tees
- **DTF** for a full-colour design you cannot screen print economically

## Print durability

Print durability depends on three things more than the method:

1. **The right ink for the fabric.** Polyester blends need low-bleed inks so dye does not move into the print.
2. **Correct curing.** Under-cured ink cracks and washes out.
3. **Care.** Washing inside out in cool water helps any print last longer.

Always wash-test a printed sample before you approve bulk.
