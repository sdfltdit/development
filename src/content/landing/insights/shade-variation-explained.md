## What shade variation is

Dyeing is chemistry, and chemistry is never perfectly repeatable. Two batches of fabric dyed to the same recipe come out a touch different. Even one roll can shift slightly from edge to centre.

That small difference is shade variation. Shoppers rarely notice it between two separate tees. They notice it at once when the sleeve of a hoodie is lighter than the body.

The goal is not a perfect match. It is keeping variation inside limits you agreed, and making sure it never shows inside one garment.

## The four types of shading

| Type | What happens | Where it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lot to lot | Two dye batches differ | Between deliveries or reorders |
| Roll to roll | Rolls from one batch differ slightly | Between garments in one order |
| Side to centre | The edges of a roll differ from the middle | Between panels cut side by side |
| End to end | The start of a roll differs from the end | Between panels cut far apart on one roll |

Side-to-centre and end-to-end shading are the dangerous ones. If cutters lay panels for one garment across the width or length of a shaded roll, the garment comes out two-toned.

## Why shade variation happens

- **Dye batch differences.** Small changes in water, temperature or timing.
- **Fabric differences.** Yarn lots, weight and tension change how dye takes.
- **Fibre mix.** Blends dye unevenly when each fibre takes dye differently.
- **Finishing.** Heat setting, softeners and washing shift colour slightly.
- **Pile fabrics.** Velour and corduroy look lighter or darker depending on which way the pile runs.

## How a shade band works

A shade band is the tool that turns "close enough" into something you can check. It usually holds three swatches:

1. **Standard.** The approved colour, usually your lab dip.
2. **Light limit.** The lightest shade you will accept.
3. **Dark limit.** The darkest shade you will accept.

Bulk rolls are compared with the shade band under set light. Rolls outside the band are rejected or used for a different order. Rolls inside it are grouped by how close they sit to the standard.

Agree the shade band in writing before bulk dyeing. It turns a subjective debate into a simple check.

## Roll-to-roll shading and cutting

Good cutting stops colour differences from showing on the garment.

- **Cut each garment from one roll.** All panels of a garment come from the same roll and, ideally, close together on it.
- **Number every panel.** Bundle tickets keep panels from one lay together through sewing.
- **Lay with the pile one way.** On velour and corduroy, every panel faces the same direction.
- **Keep shade groups apart.** Rolls from different shade groups go into different lays.

## Shade sorting before packing

Shade sorting is the final safety net. Under a light box, finished garments are grouped by shade, then packed so each carton holds one group. A store then receives pieces that match on the shelf.

Shade sorting matters most for:

- solid dark colours, where small shifts are easy to see
- sets sold together, such as a top and joggers
- uniforms, where staff stand side by side

## Metamerism

Metamerism is a special kind of colour mismatch. Two colours match in daylight but look different under shop light. It happens when different dyes are used, for example on a cotton body and a polyester zip tape.

To control it, check colours under at least two light sources when approving lab dips and bulk. Our guide to [what is a lab dip](/insights/lab-dip-explained/) explains how.

## Shade variation on reorders

Each reorder is a new dye lot. To keep it close:

- keep your approved standard swatch safe
- ask the factory to check new fabric against the original standard
- approve a new shade band for each reorder

## How shade variation counts in inspection

In a final AQL inspection, panels that differ inside one garment are usually a major defect. Pieces that fall outside the shade band are also major. Small differences inside the band are not counted. Our guide to [AQL inspection](/insights/aql-inspection-explained/) explains defect classes.
