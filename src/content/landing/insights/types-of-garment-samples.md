## The types of garment samples in brief

A garment sample is a test piece made before or during bulk. Each sample answers one question, such as "does it fit?" or "does bulk match what we approved?". Factories and buyers use many names for them, which confuses new brands.

This guide sorts the types of garment samples into the order they usually happen. It also shows which ones a small order needs, so you do not pay for rounds that add nothing.

## The sample sequence

| Stage | Sample | What it checks | Who usually asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design | Proto sample | Style, shape and construction | Every brand |
| Fit | Fit sample | Measurements and fit on the base size | Every brand |
| Selling | Salesman sample | Colours and look for showrooms and photos | Wholesale brands |
| Grading | Size set sample | Fit and measurements across sizes | Brands with wide size ranges |
| Approval | Pre-production sample | Bulk fabric, trims, labels and workmanship | Every brand |
| Bulk | TOP sample | First bulk pieces against the approved standard | Retailers and larger brands |
| Shipping | Shipment sample | Finished, packed goods from the shipment | Retailers and some importers |
| Record | Counter sample | A copy of the approved sample for comparison | Factory and buyer |

Not every order passes through every stage. Fit and proto are often the same round for simple styles.

## Proto and fit samples

The proto sample is the first time a design becomes a garment. It is often made in a similar fabric while the bulk fabric is being sourced. The aim is to judge the shape, the details and how the garment is sewn.

The fit sample comes next, or in the same round. It is made to exact measurements on the base size, usually a medium. A fit model or dress form shows where the pattern needs changing. Our guide to [garment fit problems](/insights/pattern-fit-problems-explained/) explains how to read the result.

Proto and fit rounds repeat until you approve the fit. Our guide to [3D samples](/digital-3d-sample-vs-physical-sample/) compares digital and physical sampling for this stage.

## Salesman sample

A salesman sample, sometimes called an SMS, is made for selling rather than fitting. Wholesale brands show it to store buyers, at trade fairs or in photo shoots before bulk is ordered. It usually comes in each colour, in one size.

If you sell direct to customers online, you can usually skip it. Use the pre-production sample for photos instead.

## Size set sample

A size set sample is a group of garments in several sizes, made after the pattern is graded. It checks that the fit and measurements hold across the range. A fault that looks fine in a medium can show in an XS or a 3XL.

Some buyers ask for every size. Others ask for jump sizes, such as S, M and XL, to save time. A size set matters most when:

- You sell a wide range, such as XS to 4XL or plus sizes.
- The style is fitted, such as tailoring, swimwear or leggings.
- The fabric stretches or shrinks, which changes how each size behaves.

Our guide to [garment grading](/insights/garment-grading-explained/) explains how sizes are built from the base size.

## Pre-production sample

The pre-production sample, or PP sample, is the most important one. It is made in the bulk fabric, with real trims, labels and packing. Once you approve it, it becomes the standard for the whole order.

Check it against your tech pack, measure every point and confirm branded trims. Changes after this stage cost time and money. Our [clothing sample maker](/sample-service/) page covers how we make and review it.

## TOP sample

A TOP sample means top of production. It is pulled from the first finished garments on the bulk line. The buyer or the factory compares it with the approved PP sample before the rest of the order is sewn.

TOP samples catch problems early, such as a wrong thread colour or a slipped label position. Many retailers ask for them, and they suit any brand that wants a check during bulk.

## Shipment and counter samples

A shipment sample is taken from the finished, packed order. Retailers use it to check what is arriving before the goods land.

A counter sample is a copy of an approved sample. Several are made, so each side holds the same standard. If a question comes up during bulk, everyone compares the garment against an identical piece instead of a photo.

## Which samples does your order need?

| Order | Samples to plan |
|---|---|
| First small order, simple style | Proto or fit sample, then pre-production sample |
| Wide size range or fitted style | Add a size set sample |
| Selling to wholesale buyers | Add salesman samples in each colour |
| Supplying a retailer | Add TOP and shipment samples, as their manual asks |

Every extra round adds days to your timeline. Our guide to [clothing manufacturing lead time](/guides/clothing-manufacturing-lead-time/) shows how sampling fits the full schedule.
