## Close to home or far away?

One of the first sourcing choices a brand makes is where to produce. A local factory feels safe and quick. An overseas factory promises lower prices and more capacity. Both can be the right answer, and many brands end up using both.

This guide compares the two on the points that matter, so you can decide product by product.

## Overseas vs local clothing manufacturer side by side

| Factor | Local or domestic | Overseas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price per piece | Higher | Lower on most products |
| Minimum order | Can be very small | Usually a few hundred pieces or more per style |
| Lead time | Short, no long freight | Longer, with freight and customs |
| Visiting the factory | Easy | Possible, but needs travel |
| Communication | Same language and time zone | May need planning across time zones |
| Capacity | Often limited | Usually large |
| Fabric and decoration range | Can be narrow | Often wide, made close to the factory |
| Duty | None in your own country | Depends on origin and trade deals |

## Local manufacturing pros and cons

Local manufacturing pros and cons in short:

- **Pros:** fast samples, easy visits, small runs, quick restocks and simple logistics.
- **Cons:** higher prices, limited capacity and sometimes fewer fabric, print and trim options.

A domestic clothing manufacturer suits launches, very small drops, made-to-order ranges and urgent top-ups.

## Offshore manufacturing costs

Offshore manufacturing costs are more than the factory price. To compare fairly, add:

1. **Freight,** by sea or air.
2. **Insurance.**
3. **Import duty and tax,** based on the product code and origin.
4. **Customs and delivery fees.**
5. **Sample shipping,** if the factory charges for it.

Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) walks through the sum. A lower factory price usually still wins on core products, but the gap is smaller than it looks.

## Nearshoring clothing

Nearshoring clothing means using a country close to your market. It offers shorter freight times than far overseas production, often at prices between local and far overseas. It suits fashion pieces that need speed, while far overseas factories suit basics and large runs.

## How to decide for each product

| Product type | Usual best fit |
| --- | --- |
| Core basics in volume, such as tees and hoodies | Overseas |
| Very small test runs | Local |
| Urgent restocks | Local or nearshore |
| Technical or highly decorated pieces | Wherever the skill and fabric are |
| Made-to-order or personalised items | Local |
