## Two different ways to sell clothing

**Print on demand** means a service prints your design onto a blank garment only after a customer buys it, then ships it to them. You never hold stock.

**Private label** means a factory makes your own garments in bulk, to your design, with your labels. You hold the stock and ship it yourself or through a warehouse.

Both work. They suit different stages of a brand.

## Print on demand vs private label side by side

| | Print on demand | Private label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum order | None | Factory minimum per style |
| Stock risk | None | You hold stock |
| Cost per piece | High | Lower, falls with quantity |
| Fabric and fit | Fixed by the blank | Your choice |
| Print method | Usually DTG or DTF | Any, including screen printing and embroidery |
| Labels | Limited | Your own neck labels, tags and packaging |
| Delivery to customer | Printed after order | Shipped from your stock |
| Exclusive product | No, the same blanks are everywhere | Yes |
| Upfront cash | Very low | Samples and first production |

## Print on demand margins

Print on demand margins are thinner because every piece is made one at a time. The service charges for the blank, the print, handling and often shipping. At a normal selling price, much of the money goes on the product.

That trade-off buys you something real: no stock, no cash tied up and the freedom to test many designs.

## Blank apparel and POD quality

Print on demand uses blank apparel, ready-made garments bought in bulk by the service. You choose from its list. That limits you:

- **Fabric and weight** are fixed by the blank brand.
- **Fit** is the blank's fit, not yours.
- **Labels** are usually the blank brand's, sometimes with a printed neck label added.
- **Print** is usually DTG or DTF, which suits small runs but can fade faster than screen printing.

POD quality can be good, but it is never fully yours.

## What private label adds

With private label, you choose:

- fabric, weight and colour
- fit and size chart
- print method, including screen printing, puff and embroidery
- neck labels, care labels, hang tags and packaging

That control is how a brand builds a product people come back for. Our guide to [how private label clothing works](/private-label-clothing-guide/) explains each step.

## The cost of switching

Moving to private label costs money up front:

| Line | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Samples | Proto and pre-production samples per style |
| First production | Factory minimum times price per piece |
| Freight and duty | Getting goods to your country |
| Storage and fulfilment | Holding and shipping stock |

Our guide to [clothing brand startup costs](/clothing-brand-startup-costs/) has a full budget worksheet.

## When to switch from print on demand

Knowing when to switch from print on demand matters more than which model is better. Signs it is time:

1. **One design sells steadily** month after month.
2. **You could sell the factory minimum** for that design within a season or two.
3. **Customers ask about fabric or fit**, which you cannot change on blanks.
4. **Your margin is too thin** to spend on marketing.
5. **You want your own labels and packaging** to build the brand.

Start by moving your best seller only. Keep testing new designs on print on demand.

## A hybrid approach

Many brands use both:

- **Print on demand** for new designs, limited drops and slow sellers
- **Private label** for core products and best sellers

This keeps risk low while margins on your main products improve.
