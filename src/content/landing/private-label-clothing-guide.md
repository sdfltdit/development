## What private label clothing is

Private label clothing is made by a factory and sold under your brand. The garment carries your labels, your swing tags and your packaging. The shopper never sees the factory's name.

Below you will find each stage of an order, what you control, and how this model differs from white label and print on demand.

## Private label vs white label

These two terms get mixed up. The difference is who decides what the product is.

| | Private label | White label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | Made to your design and specification | A ready product the supplier already makes |
| Fabric and fit | You choose | Fixed by the supplier |
| Exclusive to you | Yes | No, other brands sell the same item |
| Your branding | Labels, tags, packaging and design | Usually a label or print added |
| Minimum order | Factory minimum per style | Often lower |
| Time to market | Longer, because of sampling | Shorter |
| Cost per piece | Depends on your choices | Often lower at first |

White label is fast and simple. Private label takes longer but builds a product nobody else has.

## How private label clothing works, step by step

1. **Brief.** You share the garment, fabric, colours, sizes, quantity and target price.
2. **Quote.** The factory prices the style and confirms the minimum, sample fee and lead time.
3. **Tech pack and pattern.** Your tech pack becomes a pattern in your base size.
4. **Proto sample.** The first sample checks design and fit.
5. **Changes.** You comment, the factory adjusts, and a second sample follows if needed.
6. **Pre-production sample.** Made in bulk fabric with your labels and trims.
7. **Bulk production.** Fabric is cut, sewn, finished and packed.
8. **Inspection.** A final check before the balance is paid.
9. **Shipment.** Goods leave the factory on the agreed trade term.

## What you control and what the factory controls

| You | The factory |
| --- | --- |
| Design and artwork | Patterns and grading |
| Fabric and colour choices | Fabric sourcing and testing |
| Fit approval | Sample making |
| Labels, tags and packaging design | Attaching labels and packing |
| Selling price and marketing | Production planning |
| Final approvals | Quality checks and inspection |

## Brand ownership

Brand ownership is the core of private label. Protect it:

- **Register your brand name** in your main markets.
- **Keep your own files.** Patterns, tech packs and artwork should be yours.
- **Agree confidentiality.** A simple NDA covers designs and files.
- **Check labels.** Your brand, not the factory's, on every label and tag.

## Private label margins

Private label margins come from the gap between your selling price and your landed cost per piece. To work them out:

1. Get the factory price per piece.
2. Add freight, duty and clearance to get landed cost. Our guide on [how to calculate landed cost](/insights/how-to-calculate-landed-cost/) shows how.
3. Compare it with your selling price.
4. Leave room for marketing, discounts, returns and platform fees.

Private label usually gives better margins than print on demand at the same selling price, because the cost per piece falls with quantity.

## Private label checklist

Before your first order, tick off this private label checklist:

- Brand name checked and, ideally, registered
- Tech pack or clear brief for each style
- Size chart for your customer
- Fabric and colour references
- Label, tag and packaging artwork
- Budget for samples, production, freight and duty
- Written quote with price, minimum, sample fee, lead time and payment schedule
- Agreed inspection before the final payment

## When private label is not the right fit

Private label may not suit you if:

- you want to test many designs with no stock
- you cannot fund the factory minimum for each style
- you need goods within days, not weeks

In those cases, print on demand or white label can be a better first step. Our guide to [print on demand vs private label](/print-on-demand-vs-private-label/) compares them.
